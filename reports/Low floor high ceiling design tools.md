# Kernels, Not Tutorials, Make Design Tools Climbable

The "Lean / Mathematica" property turns out to be two separable things: the beginner's artifact must be the same kind of object as the expert's (one representation from the first exercise to the frontier), and something level-blind must check it on the same terms for both. Judged that way, fashion has exactly three environments where a first bodice block is structurally the professional object: the free patterns-as-code systems Seamly2D/Valentina (an interactive canvas where every point is a live formula over named measurements), FreeSewing (patterns as versioned JavaScript with a test harness that drafts every design for every body size) and GarmentCode (a typed Python DSL from ETH Zurich that four separate LLM systems now target), plus, on the 3D side, CLO and Marvelous Designer, which sell one binary at every tier from a US$8.25/month student plan to enterprise. The industrial 2D systems (Modaris, AccuMark, Optitex, Assyst) are professional and self-consistent but reach beginners only through institutional licences; the cloth simulators are Mathematica-like oracles that always evaluate and never refuse, so their strictness lives in the 2D grading rules and fabric-digitisation standards rather than in the solver. Adjacent CAD supplies the cleanest working examples of the property (Rhino + Grasshopper, Onshape, Blender) and the three canonical ways it breaks (a reduced free product, quarantined student files, a representation the professional cannot use). AI image tools for fashion produce pixels and have no ladder at all; the only AI with the property is program synthesis into a DSL or feature tree (GarmentCode, Onshape FeatureScript, Zoo's KCL, OpenSCAD via CADAM), and it is still unreliable at the top. For a technical outsider the honest starting move is Seamly2D or FreeSewing for pattern structure, CLO or Marvelous Designer for 3D, and the adoption of the professional's procedural checks from the first exercise, because no fashion kernel will force them.

*How to read the confidence tags in this report.* The research sandbox blocked direct fetches of nearly every vendor, school and press host, and the shared search budget ran out before final verification rounds. Each load-bearing claim therefore carries a tag: **[verified]** means the researchers read it directly from a primary source (GitHub, GitLab, npm, PyPI raw files) or the search tool returned the text verbatim; **[search summary]** means the fact comes from a search-engine extract of the cited page, which itself could not be opened; **[third-party]** means an aggregator, reviewer or competitor figure that conflicts with other sources or is unverified against the vendor; **[not re-read]** means a canonical paper cited bibliographically whose text was not re-read this session; **[unconfirmed]** means background knowledge or a single weak source. Every price should be re-checked against the vendor before being acted on.

## The ladder property is representation continuity plus a level-blind checker

Lean has the property for a reason that has nothing to do with pedagogy: every proof, whether a two-line game level or a research formalisation, elaborates to a term that a small trusted kernel must accept, and the kernel does not know who wrote it. Lean's own design talks describe this as de Bruijn's principle, a small trusted checker plus the option of exporting proofs to independent external checkers ([Lean PLDI slides](https://lean-lang.org/talks/LeanPLDI.pdf)) [search summary]; a 2026 applied paper puts the Lean 4 kernel at **about 5,000 lines of C++** whose job is "narrow and deterministic" ([arXiv 2604.18882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18882)) [search summary; the line count is that paper's estimate], and Lean4Lean, "an implementation of the Lean 4 kernel written in (mostly) pure Lean 4", can re-check all of Mathlib independently ([digama0/lean4lean](https://github.com/digama0/lean4lean)) [verified]. The library at the top of that ladder is large: a network study of Mathlib at a 2 February 2026 commit counts **308,129 declarations across 7,563 modules**, 79.1% of them theorems ([arXiv 2604.24797v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24797v2)) [search summary], while the community's live statistics page, as indexed, shows 136,798 definitions, 287,620 theorems and 772 contributors ([mathlib_stats](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib_stats.html)) [search summary; page proxy-blocked]; the three available counts use different classification rules, so "roughly 300k declarations and about 290k theorems in 2026" is the defensible phrasing. The on-ramps all run real Lean 4 against that same kernel: the Natural Number Game rebuilds ℕ from the Peano axioms and starts with "prove that 2 + 2 = 4" ([NNG4](https://github.com/leanprover-community/NNG4)) [verified]; Massot's *A Glimpse of Lean* promises "a feel for what proving in Lean looks like in 2 or 3 hours" on a browser server hosted by the Lean FRO ([GlimpseOfLean](https://github.com/PatrickMassot/GlimpseOfLean)) [verified]; *Mathematics in Lean* sits directly on Mathlib and presupposes no formal-methods background ([mathematics_in_lean](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathematics_in_lean)) [verified]; Mathlib's README welcomes "questions from users at all levels of expertise" into one Zulip ([mathlib4 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/README.md)) [verified]. Two caveats matter for the analogy. The first is that NNG uses a pedagogical copy of ℕ built from scratch, so its proofs are kernel-valid but not Mathlib-reusable, whereas Glimpse and MIL exercises are reusable in principle; continuity of the *checker* does not guarantee continuity of the *library*. The second is churn: Lean ships roughly monthly (v4.35.0 on 12 October 2026) ([lean4 releases](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/releases)) [verified], and de Moura's own April 2026 essay concedes that "code written two years ago probably doesn't compile today without changes" ([Why Lean?](https://leodemoura.github.io/blog/2026-4-2-why-lean/)) [search summary]. The frontier is now also machine-populated against the same kernel: Harmonic's Aristotle reached gold-medal-equivalent performance on IMO 2025 precisely because Lean provides "a reliable and fast reward signal" ([arXiv 2510.01346](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01346)) [search summary], and Lean Copilot's `suggest_tactics` and `search_proof` run inside an ordinary Lean file ([LeanCopilot](https://github.com/lean-dojo/LeanCopilot)) [verified].

Mathematica has the same shape with a weaker guarantee. Its structural source is representational monism: the Wolfram Language treats "data, formulas, code, graphics, and documents as symbolic expressions" so that one evaluator serves every level ([Wolfram principles](https://wolfram.com/language/principles/)) [search summary], which is why the *Elementary Introduction* can show a beginner that `Graphics[Circle[{0,0}]]` "is another symbolic expression, which just happens to display as a picture of a circle" ([Elementary Introduction §33](https://wolfram.com/language/elementary-introduction/33-expressions-and-their-structure.html)) [search summary]. There is no kernel certifying correctness, only a uniform evaluator, but the novice is never handed a reduced object model. The on-ramps are "same product, different doors": a free Cloud Basic tier with **200 MB and 5,000 Cloud Credits a month** that forbids "teaching a course, group research, or developing a commercial product" ([Wolfram support 53990](https://support.wolfram.com/53990)) [search summary]; a free non-production Engine for developers since 2019 ([Wolfram launch post](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/?p=20600)) [search summary; terms not re-checked for 2026]; and the full Wolfram 14.3, notebooks included, installable on a Raspberry Pi with `sudo apt install wolfram-engine`, "free, for noncommercial use" ([Wolfram Raspberry Pi](https://wolfram.com/raspberry-pi)) [search summary]. Student pricing exists but the 2026 dollar figures were not confirmed from Wolfram's page ([Wolfram student pricing](https://www.wolfram.com/mathematica/pricing/students/)) [search summary; secondary figures of US$69.95/yr and US$140 are undated]. The ladder breaks on licence, not structure, at the moment use becomes organisational or commercial.

MATLAB is the third reference point because its break is purely contractual. The free Onramp runs the professional interpreter in a browser and campus licences make the full product free to most engineering students, so a student's `.m` file is the professional artifact; but MathWorks confirms that the 2026 Home licence is a 12-month right of use ([MATLAB Answers](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/answers/2183326-home-license-in-2026)) [search summary], that "as of January 1, 2026, Perpetual Student and Home offerings have been sunset" ([MATLAB Central](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/discussions/general/886260-change-in-home-and-student-licenses)) [search summary], and third-party pages put Home at **US$165/year** with no revenue or employer work permitted ([mtopython](https://mtopython.com/learn/matlab-license-cost-2026)) [third-party; MathWorks' own pricing page was blocked]. GNU Octave 11 (February 2026) maintains a free rung of the same ladder with an explicit "Matlab compatibility" section in its release notes ([Octave NEWS.11](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gnu-octave/octave/default/etc/NEWS.11.md)) [verified]. The comparison set sharpens the test. Processing and p5.js have it by construction, a sketch being an ordinary Java or JavaScript program ([processing4](https://github.com/processing/processing4); [p5.js](https://github.com/processing/p5.js)) [verified]; Logo's stated design was "low threshold and no ceiling" ([Logo Foundation history](https://el.media.mit.edu/logo-foundation/what_is_logo/history.html)) [search summary]; Scratch deliberately traded it away, Resnick arguing that "it's not enough to provide a single path from low floor to high ceiling; we need to provide wide walls" ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/articles/lifelong-kindergarten-how-to-learn-like-a-kid-by-the-co-creator-of-scratch/)) [search summary] and, per secondary accounts, saying his group "compromised on the high ceiling" ([Brander](https://gordonbrander.com/notes/low-floor-wide-walls-high-ceiling/)) [third-party; primary not retrieved], with the block-to-text representational shift documented by Weintrop and Wilensky ([TOCE 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3089799)) [not re-read]. Jupyter has the property at the language level but not the artifact level: the large-scale reproducibility study found only about a quarter of 1.4 million GitHub notebooks executed without error and about 4% reproduced their results ([Pimentel et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/MSR.2019.00077)) [not re-read; percentages from memory, verify before quoting]. Spreadsheets are the archetypal glass ceiling, with users "generally constrained to one level, that of end-user development" ([Miller](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/41153/4/spreadsheet-paradigm-basis%20%282%29.pdf)) [search summary] and the formula-to-VBA step a known discontinuity ([Peyton Jones et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1145/944705.944721)) [not re-read].

The pedagogy and expertise literatures name the two halves independently and add a third condition. Myers, Hudson and Pausch characterise tools by "threshold and ceiling" ([TOCHI 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/344949.344959)) [search summary]; Resnick and Silverman's construction-kit principles supply "low floor and wide walls" ([IDC 2005](https://doi.org/10.1145/1109540.1109556)) [search summary]. The continuity half is the learning-science contribution: Kalyuga's expertise-reversal effect shows that "the relative effectiveness of different learning conditions reverses with changes in the level of learner expertise", so novice aids must be removable rather than baked into a different product ([Kalyuga 2007](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/EDC608/Kalyuga2007_Article_ExpertiseReversalEffectAndItsI.pdf)) [search summary]; Carroll and Carrithers' "training wheels" study found novices did better when advanced functions were blocked inside the real product rather than given a different one ([CACM 1984](https://doi.org/10.1145/358198.358218)) [not re-read]. This is the research form of the coaching analogy: the specificity-of-practice hypothesis holds that practised patterns persist and transfer "may be harmed after extensive practice" ([UFMG overview](https://repositorio.ufmg.br/items/4e4eaa42-322f-4236-904c-e5d9dc8bb4b2)) [search summary], and a scoping review of 135 motor-learning studies found positive transfer in only 36%, with 14% negative ([SCAPPS](https://scapps.org/jems/index.php/1/article/view/2548)) [search summary], which is why the early movement pattern should already be the expert pattern; no source directly tests "early bad habits are hard to unlearn", so that remains the nearest evidence. The third condition is rung size: software users adopt expert techniques "nearly exclusively or not at all", so switching "involves a global change by the user, rather than an incremental change" ([ACM 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3119928)) [search summary], Blackwell's attention-investment model explains why people rationally stop at the first automatic plateau ([Blackwell 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/HCC.2002.1046335)) [not re-read], and Ericsson's "arrested development" describes the result ([Ericsson 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2008.00227.x)) [not re-read]. The user's "Attention Is All You Need" analogy fits exactly here: the attention equation is trivial to apply to one query and one key, and the transformer is that same primitive composed; the test for a design tool is whether the primitive a beginner touches (a point placed by a formula, a sketch constraint, a stitched edge) is the primitive the expert composes, or a stand-in that must later be unlearned.

Condensed, the researchers' checklist has eight criteria, of which the first two are decisive and the rest describe exposure and economics: one object type from first exercise to frontier; a level-blind checker; progressive disclosure inside one product rather than a separate beginner product; artifacts that stay valid, reusable and upstreamable; a ceiling raised from inside the same language; a canonical graded curriculum with wide walls; one community spanning levels; and licensing that does not force a tool switch at the professional transition. The "kernel" analogue in a design field is whatever institution finally decides the artifact is real: the pattern that cuts and sews, the model that passes the geometry kernel, the shader that compiles. The table below is the typology this report applies.

| Kernel type | What it does with a novice's mistake | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Proof kernel | Refuses the artifact | Lean (C++ kernel, Lean4Lean) |
| Uniform evaluator | Evaluates to a well-formed, possibly wrong, expression | Wolfram Language, MATLAB/Octave |
| Geometry kernel plus constraint solver | Evaluates; fails loudly on impossible booleans or over-constrained sketches | Parasolid + D-Cubed (Onshape, SolidWorks, NX, Vectorworks), OCCT (FreeCAD, CadQuery, build123d), ShapeManager (Inventor, Fusion), Granite (Creo), Rhino NURBS |
| Formula or program over measurements, plus CI | Evaluates for any body; CI type-checks across sizes but does not prove assembly | Seamly2D/Valentina (QMuParser), FreeSewing (JavaScript + tests), GarmentCode (Python DSL + simulator) |
| Cloth physics | Always evaluates; errors become wrinkles, puckers or shortened seams | CLO/Marvelous Designer, Browzwear, Style3D, Optitex 3D, Blender |
| Pixels | Nothing to check | AI image generators (Raspberry, Midjourney, Firefly fills) |

## Fashion 2D: three free systems treat a pattern as a formula, the industry treats it as drawn geometry

The industrial tier is professional and self-consistent, but its representation is drawn geometry with grading expressed as per-point rule tables, and its beginner on-ramp is institutional. Lectra sells Modaris on 1-to-3-year subscriptions with no published price and names V2026.1 as current ([Lectra Modaris](https://www.lectra.com/en/fashion/products/modaris)) [search summary]; its grading methods are point-to-point, table and formula, and "parametric" behaviour is the premium Expert edition's selling point, marketed as letting patternmakers "error-proof the pattern creation process" (same page, vendor claim). An academic comparison classifies Modaris as a "hybrid CAD system that utilises a track-based method for parametric construction", as opposed to the fully parametric Seamly2D/Valentina ([Gill et al. 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17543266.2023.2260829)) [search summary]. Third-party prices conflict so badly (US$6,000 one-time, US$2,770/year, "contact sales") that none should be quoted; the same is true of AccuMark (US$389–599/month per one low-quality source) and Optitex (US$3,000 per seat to US$12,000–25,000 per module configuration, depending on which unverified guide one reads) [third-party]. Access for a beginner runs through schools: Lectra claims partnerships with **over 850 fashion schools** and a Privilege Partner network including Parsons, FIT, Esmod and Donghua ([Innovation in Textiles](https://www.innovationintextiles.com/teaching-tomorrows-fashion-talents-with-lectra/)) [search summary], at some of which students may install the suite on their own PCs for the duration of enrolment ([Modartech](https://www.modartech.com/en/lectras-education-program/)) [search summary]; Optitex licences "must be purchased by departments" ([Baylor](https://helpdeskplus.web.baylor.edu/optitex)) [search summary]; FIT's non-credit AccuMark PDS certificate runs 24 October to 12 December 2026 for **US$1,350** ([FIT CCPS](https://continuinged.fitnyc.edu/search/publicCourseSearchDetails.do?method=load&courseId=1075055)) [search summary]. Where schools document their tools, they teach the incumbents: FIT's technical-design sequence pairs "Gerber Accumark PDS and 3D virtual fitting in Browzwear" ([FIT catalog](https://catalog.fitnyc.edu/undergraduate/courses/tc/)) [search summary], Polimoda's labs run Lectra 2D and 3D with an A0 digitiser ([Polimoda](https://www.polimoda.com/student-life/facilities/)) [search summary], Parsons' Fall 2025 Digital Patternmaking course uses Modaris including its 3D feature ([New School catalogue](https://courses.newschool.edu/courses/PSOF2130)) [search summary; the 2D researcher could not independently confirm Parsons' vendor], and Central Saint Martins' published course pages name no pattern CAD package at all ([CSM BA Fashion Design: Print](https://www.arts.ac.uk/subjects/fashion-design/undergraduate/ba-hons-fashion-design-print-csm)) [search summary]. None of the schools' pages mention Seamly2D, Valentina or FreeSewing. The only self-serve industrial rungs under US$50 a month are PAD System, whose **US$15/month Basic tier is the same engine as the US$45 Standard tier** with a 20-piece cap and grading in both ([PAD pricing](https://www.padsystem.com/content/pricing)) [search summary], and TUKAcad's Learning Edition from US$19/month, which is deliberately walled: 14 pieces per file, 4 graded sizes, no DXF import or export, and "files saved in the Learning Edition will not open in the Professional Edition" ([TUKAweb plan info](https://www.tukaweb.com/subscriptions/educational_plan_info)) [search summary; the Professional price is listed as US$99 on one official page and US$199 on another]. A learner's block in TUKAcad LE is a dead end by design, the clearest structural break in the whole fashion survey.

Three open systems represent a pattern as a formal object instead. Seamly2D (the 2017 fork of Valentina) is GPLv3 software whose every point is placed by distance and angle expressed as a formula over named measurements and earlier objects, evaluated by the QMuParser engine, with patterns that "can read multi-size measurement files for standardized sizes *and* read individual measurement files for custom-fit" ([Seamly2D README](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/blob/develop/.github/README.md)) [verified]; Gill et al. note the trade: "the parametric approach, although slower to construct, offers editable blocks for efficient modification" ([Gill et al. 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17543266.2023.2260829)) [search summary]. This is the Mathematica-like door into fashion: a drafting canvas where a novice's first skirt is already a measurement-driven, regradable object. Its weak rungs are portability and professional evidence. A moderator states of the `.sm2d` format that "there is no official spec, and the schemas can and do change at any time" ([Seamly forum](https://forum.seamly.io/t/looking-for-official-documentation-or-xml-schema-for-sm2d-files/15403)) [search summary], the two forks' schemas have diverged so that post-fork Valentina files may not open in Seamly2D ([Seamly forum](https://forum.seamly.io/t/get-patterns-from-valentina-to-seamly/6417)) [search summary], and professional use is self-reported in the project's own forum, by a bespoke jeans maker and a 30-year made-to-measure tailor who adopted it for its multi-size grading ([Seamly forum](https://forum.seamly.io/t/do-you-manage-to-work-professionally-in-seamly2d/6277)) [search summary]. Valentina itself remains alive: GitLab shows v1.1.1 released 18 September 2026, with the README recommending the development branch "for professional use due to its more advanced functionality" ([Valentina releases](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina/-/releases); [Valentina README](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina/-/blob/develop/README.md)) [verified].

FreeSewing is the most Lean-like system a motivated beginner can actually enter today. A design is a JavaScript constructor whose `draft()` receives `{ Point, points, Path, paths, Snippet, snippets, options, measurements, macro, sa, complete, paperless, store, part }` and computes points and Bezier paths from a named measurement set ([FreeSewing design guide](https://freesewing.dev/guides/designs/)) [search summary]; garments literally inherit from blocks in code, Aaron's front part declaring `from: base` imported from `@freesewing/brian`, the menswear body block, and defining its own ease as a percentage with bounds ([aaron/src/front.mjs](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/designs/aaron/src/front.mjs)) [verified]. The catalogue lists blocks as first-class designs among 63 entries ([designs.json](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/config/software/designs.json)) [verified]. The checker is continuous integration: every design ships a shared test that instantiates the pattern for every adult size in `@freesewing/models` and for the doll (one-tenth) and giant (three-times) body families and fails if drafting logs any error ([tests/designs/drafting.mjs](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/tests/designs/drafting.mjs)) [verified], and the best-practices guide codifies scale invariance ("refrain from using absolute values ... embrace percentages") ([FreeSewing best practices](https://freesewing.dev/guides/best-practices/)) [search summary]. The project is live, `@freesewing/core` 4.10.2 having been published on 13 September 2026 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@freesewing/core)) [verified], though development moved from GitHub to Codeberg, whose pages were unreachable, so the documentation quoted is at least 18 months behind the code in version terms ([freesewing on GitHub](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing)) [verified]. The floor is a Node 20 toolchain and `npx @freesewing/new-design` rather than a canvas ([FreeSewing tutorial](https://freesewing.dev/tutorials/pattern-design/)) [search summary], and no verified example of a brand producing commercial patterns with it was found. GarmentCode is the most Lean-like in representation and the least usable: `pygarment` 2.0.2 (April 2025) provides Edge, Panel, Component and Interface types, with garment programs and body presets as YAML and Python ([PyPI pygarment](https://pypi.org/project/pygarment/)) [verified]; the repository (476 stars, MIT) states that "creating or modifying garment components requires writing code", while existing designs are adjusted through a parameter GUI ([GarmentCode](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode)) [verified]. Its component library is a dozen modules (bodice, sleeves, skirts, pants, collars, bands), its online configurator was reported "temporarily unavailable" from April 2025 on PyPI and "back online" on 3 June 2025 in the repository README [verified; current uptime unconfirmed], and its audience is graphics and machine-learning researchers. The consumer generators sit at the other extreme: Tailornova's US$29 Personal tier is PDF-only and only the US$49 Commercial tier exports DXF-AAMA ([Tailornova pricing](https://tailornova.com/price)) [search summary], PatternLab London sells blocks from £4.99 as PDF, AI or SVG ([PatternLab](https://patternlab.london/home/)) [search summary], and Bootstrap Fashion emails made-to-measure PDFs for US$5–27 ([Bootstrap](https://patterns.bootstrapfashion.com/all-sewing-patterns.html)) [search summary]. Their parametric logic exists but belongs to the vendor, so nothing the user builds carries into any other system except exported geometry: very low floor, zero ceiling in the relevant sense.

No mainstream 2D system has a kernel in the Lean sense. Practitioners define the human check, "walking" one piece along its partner so that "pieces sewn one-to-one should be equal" ([Minerva Patterns](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/truing-and-walking-seams-why-pattern-accuracy-matters)) [search summary]; Modaris provides an F8 seam-length spreadsheet and an assembly sheet for visual matching ([Stott textbook excerpt](https://vdoc.pub/documents/pattern-cutting-for-clothing-using-cad-how-to-use-lectra-modaris-pattern-cutting-software-1bh5c02hsdb8)) [search summary]; Optitex shows seam-allowance widths and segment lengths for all pieces in one view for the user to compare ([Optitex help](https://help.optitex.com/Optitex_2D/Viewing_the_Seam_Values_for_all_Pieces.htm)) [search summary]. Seamly2D has no built-in seam walk; a user built an empty block placed last in creation order whose lines are formulas for the difference between paired curve lengths ([Seamly forum](https://forum.seamly.io/t/a-method-to-walk-the-seams-and-check-seam-lengths/7689)) [search summary], and another matched an inside-leg seam by iterating to a 0.09 cm difference ([Seamly forum](https://forum.seamly.io/t/matching-curved-seam-lengths/3001)) [search summary]. FreeSewing's official tutorial resizes a neck curve in a `do ... while (Math.abs(delta) > 1)` loop until its length is within a millimetre of target ([FreeSewing tutorial part 3](https://freesewing.dev/tutorials/pattern-design/part3/paperless/)) [search summary]. In every system seam equality is an author-imposed invariant, not a system-enforced one; FreeSewing's CI is the closest thing to a kernel and it is a type-check (every design drafts for every body) rather than a proof of assembly, and GarmentCode's first-class `Interface` objects make it the design most amenable to a real checker, which is an inference from its type system rather than a documented feature. What the ecosystem lacks most is a Mathlib. The lowest common denominator for exchange is DXF-AAMA/ASTM with a companion `.rul` grade-rule file, which carries rules, not formulas; standard DXF carries geometry only ([Gerber community](https://gerbertechnology.vanillacommunity.com/discussion/1305/what-is-the-difference-between-a-standard-dxf-file-and-an-astm-aama-dxf-file)) [search summary], a pattern whose `.rul` is missing "will be loaded without grading" by Optitex ([Optitex import practices](https://help.optitex.com/1382687/Content/Optitex_2D/Import_best_practices_for_standard_formats.htm)) [search summary], and Grafis documents that on export "all pattern stacks are reduced to a base size with grade rules", so extreme sizes can differ after re-import ([Grafis help](https://help.grafis.de/en-gb/Content/ExportIImport/Exportformate_und_deren_Besonderheiten.htm)) [search summary]. Migration loss is therefore asymmetric: from a generator you keep geometry; from Seamly2D or FreeSewing you keep the logic in a form no industrial system reads; from TUKAcad LE you keep nothing. Grafis itself, a German construction-recording CAD that re-runs recorded drafting steps on new measurements, was identified too late to research and is the strongest unexamined candidate for "most Lean-like industrial tool" [unconfirmed].

## Fashion 3D: one simulator serves every tier, but it never says no

CLO is the fashion tool that most cleanly satisfies "beginner uses the professionals' tool simply" because there is no student edition, only a student price. The help centre lists a Student discount of **US$25/month or US$225/year**, half the Individual plan, for up to four years with verification before the trial ([CLO student discounts](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115013366988-Does-CLO-offer-student-discounts)) [search summary; undated article], an Individual plan of US$50/month or US$450/year ([CLO cost](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012815487-How-much-does-CLO-cost)) [search summary; a competitor blog's "$2,500 yearly" figure is unverified marketing], enterprise by quotation, and a 14-day trial for individuals and students only ([CLO trial](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012788967-How-do-I-start-a-CLO-free-trial-version)) [search summary]. The tiers differ in price, support and CLO-SET collaboration rather than in the modelling core, which is an inference from the plan descriptions since the feature matrix itself could not be read. The on-ramp is CLO Academy (October 2024) with an Individual User Verification Assessment for certification ([press release](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/5956633)) [search summary], and CLO's own September 2026 estimate is **two to four weeks to a simple garment and eight to twelve weeks to portfolio level**, with newcomers to fashion needing roughly an extra month ([CLO blog](https://clovirtualfashion.blog/2026/09/22/how-long-to-learn-clo-timeline/)) [search summary; vendor estimate]. The ceiling includes a Python Script API and a C++ plug-in SDK ([developer.clo3d.com](https://developer.clo3d.com/)) [search summary; scope unverified, a 2018 thread described the API as import/export-centric and a user comment claims Windows-only], DXF-AAMA/ASTM exchange with 2D pattern CAD ([CLO DXF](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000493067-2D-Pattern-DXF-Import-Export)) [search summary], and in 2026.1 a per-pattern revision history, a "2D Grading Review" across all sizes and GPU simulation by default ([CLO notice 1442](https://clo3d.com/en/resources/notices/1442)) [search summary]. Institutional licences are campus-bound (Aalto's EDU licence is for study only and its products "may not be transferred to a third party") ([Aalto](https://www.aalto.fi/en/workshops-aalto-school-of-arts-design-and-architecture/clo3d)) [search summary], which is why the individual student price matters.

Marvelous Designer is the same kernel at a lower price with the production boundary removed. The vendor states that "the core technology behind the solutions are the same", that MD serves CG, animation and games, and that CLO-only features are the tech pack, DXF-AAMA/ASTM import/export and avatar size adjustability ([MD support](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358306734361-There-is-another-similar-software-called-CLO-what-is-the-difference)) [search summary]. Personal pricing is US$39/month or US$280/year, enterprise US$199/month or US$2,000/year since 2 December 2025 ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/11/clo-virtual-fashion-releases-marvelous-designer-2025-2/)) [search summary], and since 9 July 2026 students pay **US$8.25/month** over a four-year window, with three free "unrestricted" months for new sign-ups until 31 October 2026 ([AAP press release](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260709ae01014/)) [search summary; feature or commercial restrictions on the student plan unconfirmed]. A student who starts on MD can move to CLO without relearning the simulation layer; the discontinuity is at production (no DXF-AAMA, no tech pack), not at the physics. Browzwear is the enterprise dialect. Its February 2026 pricing change created the first standing self-serve path for a solo learner: **Freelancer US$75/month or US$750/year**, Freelancer Plus US$150/US$1,500, Teams US$3,950 per user per year, Enterprise by quote ([Browzwear pricing](https://browzwear.com/blog/browzwear-pricing-plan)) [search summary; whether Freelancer includes Lotta, Stylezone and the fabric library is unconfirmed, and no student price was found]. Browzwear University offers free 101, 201 and 301 courses plus a paid membership ([BWU](https://university.browzwear.com/); [BWU membership](https://browzwear.com/blog/bwu-updates-and-membership-2023)) [search summary]. Its strongest claim to a self-consistency core is measured physics: the Fabric Analyzer captures "the full force extension curve of a fabric sample" and translates it into mass, thickness, bend, stretch, linearity and shear, exchanged through the open U3M format ([Browzwear FAB](https://browzwear.com/products/fabric-analyzer)) [search summary], and the Tension Map colours stress "based on the physics of the fabric" ([Browzwear tension map](https://help.browzwear.com/en/articles/13854342-tension-map)) [search summary]. No public API documentation or developer portal was found; the "Open Platform" is described through an FNX integration ([Browzwear FNX](https://browzwear.com/blog/browzwear-fnx-integration)) [search summary]. Style3D, which raised about US$100 million in 2022 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2022/3d-fashion-firm-linctex-raises-100m/)) [search summary] and has owned Assyst since January 2023 ([The Interline](https://www.theinterline.com/?p=16945)) [search summary], publishes almost no first-party price list; its own blogs give student figures of US$150/year or "around $15/month" and Studio tiers anywhere from US$35 to US$299/month ([Style3D blog](https://www.style3d.com/blog/?p=8406); [Style3D AI blog](https://www.style3d.ai/blog/what-is-the-price-of-style3d-and-is-it-worth-it/)) [third-party in effect; mutually inconsistent vendor SEO content]. The 2D-first incumbents treat 3D as a validation view: Lectra says 3D lets patternmakers "validate volumes, proportions, fit and construction logic before physical" samples ([Lectra interview](https://tech.yahoo.com/computing/articles/lectra-bridging-gap-between-creative-180000197.html)) [search summary], Optitex and Lectra 3D are quote-only, and only Tukatech sells 3D to individuals off the shelf (TUKA3D Designer Edition US$29/month with a 14-day trial) ([Tukatech](https://tukatech.com/products/tuka3d-de)) [search summary].

The decisive finding is that none of these simulators is a kernel in the Lean sense. CLO's Check Sewing Length tool highlights sewing lines whose length difference exceeds **3 mm** in red, but it is an optional tool and does not block simulation ([CLO Check Sewing Length](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012226027-Check-Sewing-Length)) [search summary]; a community thread on pocket-bag stitch lines that failed to grade with the body seam reports "puckering and stitching length not matching" in larger sizes while the simulation ran anyway, and the thread is unresolved ([CLO community](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/ja/community/posts/29039442377497-Grading-Sewing-length-issues-on-certain-pieces)) [search summary]. A Marvelous Designer course instructor sews mismatched curtain panels and tells students the mismatch "isn't a major problem, since the extra fabric simply ends up as more wrinkles" ([Lynda course](https://origin-www.lynda.com/Marvelous-Designer-tutorials/Sewing-curtain/2825488/2398596-4.html)) [search summary]. A 2026 arXiv paper observes that ease "is typically a byproduct of geometry or simulation rather than an independent design variable" ([arXiv 2606.29419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29419)) [search summary]. The simulator is thus a physical consistency oracle of the Mathematica kind: it always evaluates, sometimes to a visibly wrong object, and it leaves detection to procedural checks a beginner can skip. The practical consequence for the coaching analogy is sharp: because the kernel is permissive, the professional's "movement pattern" is the habit of running Check Sewing Length, reviewing grading across sizes, reading tension maps and using digitised fabric rather than defaults, and nothing in the tool forces a novice to acquire it. Strictness in fashion lives on the 2D side (seam-allowance and grading rules, DXF-AAMA/ASTM) and in fabric-digitisation standards (FAB/U3M, Vizoo-accredited scanning), not in the solver. Blender fits this picture as a free CG floor rather than an apparel one: sewing springs have existed in its cloth simulator since 2.70 in 2014 ([Blender task T31269](https://developer.blender.org/T31269)) [search summary], the free Seams to Sewing Pattern add-on turns a seamed mesh into a cloth-sim-ready, printable pattern ([thomaskole.nl](https://thomaskole.nl/s2s/)) [search summary], Simply Cloth Studio 2.0 (January 2026, US$36–46) and Garment Tool (US$54) add MD-style pattern-and-sew workflows ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/01/vjaceslav-tissen-releases-simply-cloth-studio-2-0-for-blender/); [Gumroad](https://bartoszstyperek.gumroad.com/l/GarmentTool?ref=311)) [search summary], but no source shows a Blender pipeline producing DXF-AAMA/ASTM patterns or consuming measured fabric data, so it connects to film and game garment work, not to apparel production. Market context tempers all of this: The Interline's analysis of CLO's October 2026 EU Summit reports that "digital twin momentum felt like it was slowing" and that CLO executives linked user feedback to "demand for better 2D CAD capabilities" ([The Interline](https://www.theinterline.com/2026/10/15/fresh-pressure-same-promise-analysing-the-clo-eu-summit-2026/)) [search summary]; a Kalypso survey cited there found "only 4% of companies have actually made significant changes to their calendars" after building 3D capability ([The Interline](https://www.theinterline.com/2026/10/13/realigning-3d-and-ai-with-speed-to-market/)) [search summary; survey year not stated]; and Kalypso names "lack of talent and expertise (53%)" as the leading barrier ([Kalypso](https://kalypso.com/viewpoints/entry/research-results-discussion-adoption-of-3d-digital-product-creation-in-reta)) [search summary]. Job postings make CLO the lingua franca ("Proficiency in CLO 3D is mandatory ... Browzwear experience is preferred") ([Tuoi Tre jobs](https://vieclam.tuoitre.vn/en/search-job/3d-apparel-designer.35C80B4E.html)) [search summary], and a systematic review of twenty studies of CLO3D in vocational education names "lack of teacher training (65% of studies)" as the main barrier ([ResearchGate 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388958182_The_use_of_CLO3D_application_in_vocational_school_fashion_expertise_program_Innovations_challenges_and_recommendations)) [search summary], consistent with the bottleneck being pattern knowledge rather than the tool.

## Adjacent CAD: Rhino, Onshape and Blender show the ladder, SketchUp, NX Student and OpenSCAD show three ways it breaks

Rhino plus Grasshopper is the strongest architecture-side example and the one with a studied ceiling. The educational single-user licence is **US$195, one-time**, and is "the same as commercial ones except that they cannot be transferred to another user", with a reseller stating commercial work is allowed ([McNeel sales](https://www.rhino3d.com/sales/pacific/Australia/NSW)) [search summary]; commercial Rhino 8 is US$995 perpetual ([RenderAHouse](https://www.renderahouse.com/blog/rhino-pricing)) [third-party]; Grasshopper 2 ships inside the Rhino 9 work-in-progress builds ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/rhino-9-wip-available-now/180749)) [search summary; Rhino's in-house NURBS kernel and Grasshopper's bundling history unconfirmed here]. The pedagogical evidence is the best in the whole survey. Celani and Vaz found that "with novice students better results were obtained with the visual programming language", while "scripting ... was used by advanced students to implement rule-based generative systems" ([Celani & Vaz 2012](https://papers.cumincad.org/cgi-bin/works/paper/ijac201210108)) [search summary]; a later teaching paper summarises the comparisons as showing visual algorithm construction is "perceptually limited" and "more successful in relatively simple and pedagogical uses" ([Open House International](https://www.emerald.com/ohi/article/49/4/736/1212363/Teaching-parametric-design-fostering-algorithmic)) [search summary]; Davis's thesis documents that parametric models in practice "often can't absorb certain design changes", leaving the designer to rebuild or abandon ([Davis 2013](https://danieldavis.com/thesis)) [search summary]; and Aish and Woodbury's 2005 argument that designers work at two levels, "definition of schemata and constraints; and search within a schema collection for meaningful instances", is the earliest explicit case for multiple notations over one constraint model ([Aish & Woodbury](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11536482_13)) [search summary]. The escape hatch from the visual ceiling (scripting components, Grasshopper 2) sits inside the same canvas, so the move from simple to expert is a change of notation, not of tool. Dynamo reproduces this ladder inside Revit, nodes to DesignScript code blocks to Python importing the Revit API ([Dynamo Primer](https://primer.dynamobim.org/10_Custom-Nodes/10-5_Python-Revit.html)) [search summary], and this "one data model, several notations of increasing power" pattern is the clearest progressive-disclosure design in the field. The BIM tools all pass structurally and fail contractually: Autodesk Education is free for one year, "renewable annually as long as you're eligible", and "shall not be used for commercial, professional or any other for-profit purposes" ([Autodesk Education](https://www.autodesk.com/education/home)) [search summary] against a Revit list price of about US$3,005/year ([RenderAHouse](https://www.renderahouse.com/blog/revit-pricing)) [third-party]; Archicad's free education licence produces files that carry an irremovable "Educational" watermark ([Graphisoft](https://www.graphisoft.com/en-ee/education/)) [search summary]; Vectorworks' free Design Suite is non-commercial and SheerID-verified ([Vectorworks](https://www.vectorworks.net/newsroom/academic-offers-resources)) [search summary]. SketchUp's plateau is manufactured: the free tier is a different product, web-only, non-commercial, without DWG/DXF import, extensions or LayOut, against Go at US$129, Pro at US$399 and Studio at US$819 per year ([Vagon](https://vagon.io/blog/sketchup-pricing); [AECO Space](https://www.aeco.space/en/blog/sketchup-pro-vs-free-what-are-the-main-differences)) [third-party].

In mechanical CAD the self-consistency core is literally shared, which makes the continuity question unusually concrete. Parasolid, developed by Shape Data and now owned by Siemens, and the D-Cubed constraint solvers are licensed to "practically every CAD company"; SolidWorks and Onshape "both use the Parasolid kernel" ([engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/parasolid-d-cubed-and-siemens-the-heart-of-your-cad-software-belongs-to-another/)) [search summary], and Siemens lists Autodesk, Ansys, Bentley, Nemetschek, SolidWorks, Onshape and Shapr3D among licensees ([Siemens Parasolid](https://www.siemens.com/en-us/products/plm-components/parasolid/)) [search summary]; Vectorworks names D-Cubed 2D DCM "the industry standard geometric constraint solver for parametric sketching" ([Vectorworks partner page](https://www.vectorworks.net/en-US/community/partner-network/find-partner/siemens-digital-industries-software)) [search summary]. Autodesk's ShapeManager was forked from ACIS 7.0 in 2001 for Inventor and other Autodesk products ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShapeManager)) [search summary; Fusion's use of it widely reported but unconfirmed here], Creo runs PTC's in-house Granite, and FreeCAD, CadQuery and build123d all run on Open CASCADE ([Hysen Labs](https://hysenlabs.com/projects/gumyr-build123d)) [search summary]. A beginner who learns sketch constraints in any Parasolid/D-Cubed tool is using the professional's solver; the kernels differ in robustness more than expressiveness. Onshape is the cleanest "same system from novice to enterprise": the Free plan (public, non-commercial documents) and the free Student and Educator plans, where "eligibility is based on educational use — not email domain", differ from the US$1,500 Standard and US$2,500 Professional tiers only in privacy and add-on modules ([Onshape education plans](https://www.onshape.com/en/blog/education-plans-overview-student-educator-enterprise); [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/onshape/pricing)) [search summary; third-party for prices]. Fusion is free for hobbyists earning under US$1,000 a year from their designs but caps them at **10 active editable documents**, disables simulation and generative design, and in 2020 removed DWG, DXF, IGES and SAT export from the personal tier, restoring STEP only after protest ([StackSheriff](https://stacksheriff.com/3d-printing/fusion-360-free-personal-license/?format=md); [Fabbaloo](https://www.fabbaloo.com/?p=194461)) [third-party; search summary]. SolidWorks Design Standard became free for students on 1 July 2026 with no .edu email required ([SolidProfessor](https://solidprofessor.com/?p=9937267)) [search summary], against commercial prices of US$2,820–4,716 per year ([ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-solidworks-cost/)) [third-party]. NX Student Edition is free and the same program, but output is watermarked and "part files created in NX Student Edition cannot be retrieved in a commercial version of NX" ([Siemens NX blog](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/nx-design/nx-student-edition-free-download/)) [search summary], the file-quarantine pattern. The open ladder is FreeCAD 1.1 (24 March 2026) with Python everywhere and Open CASCADE's documented fillet and boolean fragility ([Wikipedia FreeCAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreeCAD); [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/FreeCAD-1.1-Released)) [search summary], CadQuery and build123d, which practitioners find intuitive "once a user got past its setup" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41548945)) [search summary], and OpenSCAD, whose Manifold backend became the default for snapshot users in August 2025 ([OpenSCAD list](https://lists.openscad.org/empathy/thread/TMJEJCZINIJNYJX2YF7IDNBAPQY66KIF)) [search summary] but whose output is a mesh. OpenSCAD is the representation-break case: Lean-like as a language, exact and declarative, yet the formal system a beginner learns does not carry the B-rep faces, fillets and STEP semantics a professional needs, which is exactly why build123d sits on OCCT at the cost of a harder floor. The lesson for a textual pattern language is direct: it must keep the professional's geometry (true surfaces, seam allowances as offsets) or it will plateau the way OpenSCAD does. The consumer-to-professional move in this domain rarely breaks on geometry and usually on one of four things: export or document gates (Fusion personal), file quarantine (NX Student, Archicad), a representation the pro tool cannot read (Tinkercad's primitives export to Fusion only if built from primitives ([Western Thunder forum](https://westernthunder.me.uk/threads/smoothing-curved-output-from-tinkercad.12541)) [search summary]), or the mental model itself, students finding the Tinkercad-to-Fusion switch hard "because we had to think in 2D before moving to 3D" ([JMU course blog](https://sites.lib.jmu.edu/isci104-spring2020/?p=2589)) [search summary], the sketch-then-feature paradigm being the real hurdle.

General 3D supplies the purest free case and the best governance example. Blender 5.0 (19 November 2025) is "the latest major version of the open-source 3D software for VFX, animation, game development and visualization" and its Geometry Nodes gained volume grids, bundles and closures ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/11/blender-5-0-is-out-check-out-its-5-key-features)) [search summary]; 5.2 LTS (14 July 2026) added an experimental Cloth Dynamics node, with the classic Cloth modifier still the stable path ([BlenderDeluxe](https://blenderdeluxe.com/en/3d-design/cloth-simulation-in-blender-52-from-the-cloth-modifier-to-the-cloth-dynamics-node-1115)) [search summary], and paid and free Geometry Nodes toolboxes now produce procedural hems, seams, topstitching and puckering aimed at mirroring MD, CLO and Substance ([Procedural Sewing Toolbox](https://alexandrealbisser.gumroad.com/l/ProceduralSewingToolbox)) [search summary]. The novice's free install is byte-identical to the studio's, and Geometry Nodes gives the same nodes-first, code-when-needed ladder as Grasshopper; what Blender offers fashion is finish and presentation rather than pattern engineering. Houdini gives away a full procedural kernel as Apprentice and sells Indie at **US$299/year under a US$100,000 revenue cap** ([SuperRenders](https://superrendersfarm.com/article/how-much-does-houdini-cost)) [third-party; SideFX's own page unreachable, Apprentice format and resolution limits unconfirmed], a model of low floor with explicit revenue-indexed ceilings whose restricted output formats are the mechanism that makes early work non-portable. p5.js 2.0 (2025) is a live example of a low-floor tool managing its own breaking change: 1.x "will continue to be supported until August, 2026" and compatibility add-ons restore removed features ([p5.js-compatibility](https://github.com/processing/p5.js-compatibility)) [verified], a practice any fashion tool that wants to stay "one system" across versions should copy. The crossover into fashion follows the kernel: FIT calls Rhino "the foremost application used in jewelry" ([FIT](https://www.fitnyc.edu/academics/academic-divisions/ccps/noncredit/rhino-grasshopper-3d.php)) [search summary], Rhino with Grasshopper "is widely used for parametric last design, sole geometry" with "graded size variants" for footwear ([CadWax](https://cadwax.co.uk/blogs/news/rhino-3d-for-product-design)) [search summary], and PAACADEMY teaches "scaling parametric logic from jewelry-scale ornaments to full garment systems" ([PAACADEMY](https://paacademy.com/course/dynamic-wearables-for-fashion-design-2-0)) [search summary]. Hard goods (jewellery, heels, soles, lasts) moved to Rhino because free-form NURBS and graded variants fit them; soft goods did not move to any general CAD because none represents a flat pattern with seam allowances and grain as a first-class object, and no documented use of Onshape, Fusion or FreeCAD in bags or apparel was found.

## AI tools: program synthesis keeps the ladder, image generation has none

Apply the central test, whether the tool emits a structured artifact that lives in a professional formal system or only pixels that a human must redraw, and the AI fashion design category fails as a whole. Raspberry AI, the best-funded entrant with a **US$24 million Series A led by Andreessen Horowitz** in January 2025 and customers including Under Armour and Li & Fung ([mind Retail](https://mind.eu.com/retail/en/article/nrf-2025-raspberry-raises-us24-million-to-put-ai-at-the-service-of-sustainability)) [search summary], integrated with Browzwear by passing images in and exporting AI concepts back "as resources or colorway references attached to existing 3D garments" ([Raspberry press release](https://www.raspberry.ai/press/raspberry-ai-and-browzwear-announce-integration-to-connect-generative-ai-and-3d-workflows)) [search summary], a tacit statement that no geometry crosses the boundary. The New Black exports SVG flat sketches and drafts tech packs ([The New Black](https://thenewblack.ai/ai-design-features/ai-clothing-sketch-technical-generator)) [search summary] and is unusually candid that its studio "does not grade. A design is one size, drawn on one body" and that "the size run, the tolerances and the way a pattern is cut are a patternmaker's work" ([The New Black guide](https://thenewblack.ai/blog/what-is-ai-fashion-design)) [search summary]. Vizcom, with over US$52 million raised, states in its own hiring copy that softgoods "(fabric, stitching, seams) can render convincingly but still don't translate cleanly into patterns or knit structures" ([Vizcom job listing](https://remotivated.com/jobs/research-engineer-design-to-manufacturing-at-vizcom)) [search summary], the most honest admission in the category and evidence the problem is unsolved in its product. Adobe's Firefly features in Illustrator produce pattern swatches and vector fills, not garment structure ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/il_en/products/illustrator/pattern-generator.html)) [search summary], and a peer-reviewed study finds Midjourney "restricted to facilitating early fashion design stages" ([Zhang & Liu 2024](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/unlocking-the-potential-of-artificial-intelligence-in-fashion-design-and-e-commerce-applications-the-case-of-midjourney)) [search summary]. Resleeve, Refabric, Fashable and Ablo are image or merch-graphic generators whose pricing is third-party and conflicting; Botto, which the brief asked to check, is a community-governed AI art project by Mario Klingemann with no fashion connection ([NeurIPS 2022 demo](https://nips.cc/virtual/2022/63193)) [search summary]. The documented 2026 pipeline is therefore generate concepts in Midjourney, Firefly, Raspberry or The New Black, then rebuild the garment by hand in CLO, Browzwear or Style3D: a practitioner guide puts it as "an AI-generated design is a picture. A factory — or a single tailor — works from a spec" ([Made Apt](https://www.madeapt.com/guides/ai-design-to-tech-pack)) [search summary], and a service market prices the gap, with Binary Cloth rebuilding AI designs as CLO or MD garments with graded patterns and tech packs **from US$39 per garment** ([Binary Cloth](https://binarycloth.com/service/ai-fashion-design-service/)) [search summary]. The broken link is representational: an image carries no body, no measurement, no seam topology and no fabric parameters, so everything downstream has to re-derive them.

Structured output exists in two places: inside the professional suites and in the research line. CLO 2026.0's "3D Pencil (Avatar)" lets a user sketch on the avatar and convert the strokes into pattern pieces, with a conversion preview ([CLO 3D Pencil](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/55461410696345-3D-Pencil-Avatar)) [search summary], and its Pattern Drafter generates points of measure and grading from entered measurements ([CLO features](https://clo3d.com/en/clo/features)) [search summary]; CLO does not describe either as machine learning. Browzwear's Auto Arrange and Stitch reached general availability on 29 April 2026 but only for "select enterprise license holders with an active Stylezone site", and "freelancer licenses do not support Auto Stitch" ([Browzwear release notes](https://help.browzwear.com/en/articles/14794274-vstitcher-2026-1-2-release-notes); [Auto Stitch help](https://help.browzwear.com/en/articles/13229692-auto-stitch)) [search summary]. Style3D is the incumbent that has shipped a learned path: GarmageNet (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025) generates panels, stitching and simulation-ready initialisations from text, sketches, images or point clouds, with vendor-reported **99.16% precision on sewing-point identification** ([Style3D release](https://vietnamnews.vn/media-outreach/1798671/style3d-advances-fashion-ai-from-content-generation-to-connected-product-workflows.html)) [search summary; vendor figure], and the GarmageJigsaw repository states the stitch-prediction module has been integrated into Style3D Studio through its Python API, which requires a developer-preview trial licence ([garmagejigsaw-impl](https://github.com/Style3D/garmagejigsaw-impl)) [verified]. Lectra launched the agentic "Apogy" platform on 15 September 2026, with agents generating concepts and 3D renderings from sketches, images or text and tracking every change ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lectra-unveils-apogy-the-first-solution-embedding-agentic-artificial-intelligence-to-reinvent-fashion-product-development-302879189.html)) [search summary; whether it outputs patterns or connects to Modaris is unconfirmed], having taken an 18% stake in Six Atomic's modular pattern libraries in 2024 ([Textile World](https://www.textileworld.com/textile-world/knitting-apparel/2024/09/lectra-announces-a-strategic-partnership-with-six-atomic/)) [search summary]; Lectra's 2025 revenue was €507 million and Q1 2026 fell 11% like-for-like ([Lectra Q1 2026](https://www.lectra.com/sites/default/files/investors_files/Lectra_Financial%20Information_Q1%202026_EN.pdf)) [search summary]. All three keep the user inside the professional file format, which is the ladder property, but none has a low floor. Tukatech's TUKA APM, a 2019 measurement-driven automatic pattern-and-grading module ([Just-Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/tukatech-unveils-fully-automatic-pattern-making-system/)) [search summary], and Tailornova's 15-measurement engine ([Tailornova technology](https://tailornova.com/technology)) [search summary] prove that measurement-parametric generation is a solved, cheap floor without generative AI; the unsolved step is from an image or sketch to that parameter set.

That step is where GarmentCode becomes the Lean-like AI-native candidate. The research line has split into two camps. Direct geometry prediction (NeuralTailor, Sewformer, SewingLDM, GarmageNet) emits panels that still need correction: Dress-1-to-3 reports Sewformer panels "often fail to align with the input image" ([arXiv 2502.03449](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.03449)) [search summary], and GarmentImage reports IoU on unseen panel combinations of 0.31 for NeuralTailor and 0.43 for Sewformer against 0.81 for its own representation ([arXiv 2505.02592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02592)) [search summary]. Program synthesis into a DSL produces a reusable, editable, self-consistent artifact, and three independent systems chose GarmentCode as the target: Design2GarmentCode (Style3D Research and Zhejiang University, CVPR 2025) fine-tunes an LLM to write GarmentCode programs from images, text or sketches with a multimodal agent issuing corrections, reporting simulation success of 100% for text and 94% for images ([arXiv 2412.08603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08603)) [search summary], with MIT-licensed code ([design2garmentcode-impl](https://github.com/Style3D/design2garmentcode-impl)) [verified]; ChatGarment (MPI-IS, CVPR 2025) emits GarmentCode JSON from in-the-wild images and admits it "may occasionally produce garments with incorrect lengths or widths" ([ChatGarment](https://github.com/biansy000/ChatGarment)) [verified]; AIpparel (Stanford, CVPR 2025 Highlight) trains on more than 120,000 garments from GarmentCodeData ([AIpparel](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Nakayama_AIpparel_A_Multimodal_Foundation_Model_for_Digital_Garments_CVPR_2025_paper.html)) [search summary], the ECCV 2024 dataset of **115,000 made-to-measure garments with sewing patterns** generated by the GarmentCode pipeline ([ETH Research Collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/researchdata/d9df8fe3-2add-49a2-bb58-21750ca7970c)) [search summary]. An "agent-safe transactional" MCP toolset over Valentina, GarmentCode and remote Warp simulation, with preview-then-commit tokens and append-only revisions, appeared in September 2026 and describes itself as "not production-ready patternmaking certification" ([garment-cad-mcp](https://github.com/0xZoharHuang/garment-cad-mcp)) [verified]. The shape is exactly the one the question asks about: a small typed DSL whose programs compile to a JSON spec that a simulator validates, a parameter GUI for non-programmers and a code layer for experts, and now a de facto target for machine generation, the role Lean plays for AI provers. The missing rungs are practical: researcher-grade installation, a dozen components with no tailored jacket construction, knit or grading rules, LLM front-ends shipped as research code, and no product.

General CAD gives the same answer with more hands-on data. Onshape's redesigned AI Advisor shipped to every user on 14 October 2025 ([PTC](https://www.ptc.com/en/news/2025/ptc-announces-latest-onshape-ai-advisor-release)) [search summary], FeatureScript generation inside the Feature Studio followed in March 2026 ([Onshape blog](https://www.onshape.com/en/blog/ai-artificial-intelligence-cloud-native-cad-pdm-platform)) [search summary], and on 13 August 2026 the Onshape FeatureScript MCP Server connected coding LLMs to FeatureScript through Onshape Labs, drafting code, inserting it, running it and iterating on errors ([PTC](https://www.ptc.com/en/news/2026/onshape-launches-featurescript-mcp-server)) [search summary]. Zoo's changelog records that text-to-CAD first produced static files, then parametric KCL code whose error rate Zoo cut "from 50% to 16%" ([Zoo](https://zoo.dev/blog/whats-new-august)) [search summary; vendor figure]; Design Studio's point-and-click tools act on the underlying KCL so designs can live in source control, and the app is MIT-licensed with a hosted geometry engine ([KittyCAD/modeling-app](https://github.com/KittyCAD/modeling-app)) [verified]; a seven-tool test called Zoo's parametric sliders a standout but a fully specified 24-tooth gear failed ([Wevolver](https://www.wevolver.com/article/we-tested-7-text-to-cad-tools-are-they-actually-useful-for-engineers)) [search summary]. CADAM, Adam's open-source web app, has the LLM write "fully parametric OpenSCAD" compiled in-browser with adjustable dimensions exposed as sliders ([Adam-CAD/CADAM](https://github.com/Adam-CAD/CADAM)) [verified]; Backflip's copilot, generally available from 4 August 2026 from US$20/month, rebuilds scans into native feature histories ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260803007022/en/)) [search summary], and in engineering.com's test its Fast Mode "failed on all four attempts" while Thinking Mode was "as far as I could tell by eye, flawless" ([engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/backflips-back-is-the-mesh-to-cad-ai-real-this-time/)) [search summary]. CMU's CADSmith multi-agent system writes CadQuery, validates with the OpenCASCADE kernel and reports 100% execution success with mean Chamfer distance 0.74 against 28.37 zero-shot, while noting near-miss geometric errors can pass validation ([CADSmith](https://github.com/jabarkle/CADSmith)) [verified]. The mesh generators (Hunyuan3D-2 returns a trimesh as GLB/OBJ ([Hunyuan3D-2](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2)) [verified], Meshy, Tripo, Spline, Autodesk's non-commercial Project Bernini) emit meshes, and the research fixes such as CADFit are literally mesh-to-program converters ([CADFit](https://github.com/ghadinehme/CADFit)) [verified]. The ladder property is strongest where the GUI and the code are two views of one artifact, Zoo, Onshape and CADAM, because a beginner's slider tweak and an expert's code edit touch the same object; the ceiling is low on reliability, which is tolerable only because the output lands in the professional's own verification environment, the kernel, rather than in a flat image. Fashion's checker is weaker than CAD's, since a garment pattern also needs grading, fabric behaviour and a fit judgement the research systems admit they get wrong, so fashion's AI ladder is likely to stay broken longer.

The pedagogical evidence lines up with the artifact test. FIT created the cross-divisional AURA committee ("AI Usage, Responsibility and Adaptation") that "sets guardrails around AI use in specific classes" ([YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2026/06/04/fashion-schools-and-students-are-reckoning-with-ai/)) [search summary]; the College for Creative Studies states that "traditional skill development will continue to be the focus for Fashion Design" ([CCS](https://campus.collegeforcreativestudies.edu/academic-affairs/?p=2927)) [search summary]; a survey of educators at CSM, LCF, Marangoni, Parsons and IFM found a "wild west" ([1 Granary](https://1granary.substack.com/p/fashion-schools-ai-problem-csm-ifm)) [search summary; paywalled beyond the introduction]. A DRS 2026 synthesis finds generative AI raises creativity and visualisation but brings higher cognitive load and "weaker awareness of production" ([DRS 2026](https://dl.designresearchsociety.org/drs-conference-papers/drs2026/researchpapers/147)) [search summary], a practitioner essay concludes that "knowing whether that image represents a good product becomes much more valuable" ([Meer](https://www.meer.com/en/111706-what-ai-really-changed-for-fashion-designers)) [search summary], and The Interline's summer 2026 survey of about 100 brands found 90% of fashion professionals use AI daily, about a quarter trust it for important decisions, and AI is perceived "least mature the closer it gets to real product development and specialist disciplines like patternmaking" ([The Interline](https://www.theinterline.com/2026/10/08/is-ai-already-good-enough-for-fashion/)) [search summary]. No source uses the phrase "all ceiling, no floor", but "weaker awareness of production" is its documented form: image tools raise the visual ceiling for novices while removing the forcing function (drafting, draping) that used to teach construction. The Interline's own framing is the strongest pro-AI case and it is scoped precisely to concept-to-shareable-visual, where "AI can offer a considerably faster and far less labour-intensive method than 3D modelling" ([Interline DPC Report 2026](https://www.theinterline.com/The-Interline-DPC-Report-2026-Beauty-Shareable.pdf)) [search summary], not concept-to-pattern.

Three patterns fall out of the table. First, the two decisive criteria sort the fashion tools more cleanly than price does: the three free formal systems and the two CLO-kernel products pass representation continuity, and nothing in fashion passes the level-blind-checker test the way Lean or even a B-rep kernel does, FreeSewing's CI being the nearest approach. Second, the licensing typology is the same across domains. The patterns that preserve the ladder are one perpetual program at two prices (Rhino), one cloud document with privacy as the paywall (Onshape), one binary with a time-limited student discount (CLO, MD), and free everywhere (Lean, Octave, FreeCAD, Blender, Seamly2D, FreeSewing, GarmentCode); the patterns that break it are a reduced product beside the professional one (SketchUp Free, Tinkercad, Fusion personal's gates, TUKAcad LE) and a full program with quarantined files (NX Student, Archicad's watermark, TUKAcad LE again). Third, the "kernel" migrates by domain: in mechanical CAD it is the geometry kernel and constraint solver and the beginner uses the professional's one; in fashion it is split between a permissive physical oracle in 3D and author-imposed invariants in 2D, with the exchange format carrying rules rather than logic.

## Recommendations: start where the first block is already the professional object

The first recommendation is a way of looking rather than a tool. Before adopting any environment, ask the two decisive questions: does the beginner's file open unchanged in the professional's tool with the same semantics, and is there something level-blind that checks it on the same terms for both? Then ask the cheaper questions in order: can the beginner mode be switched off without exporting or converting, are the power features expressed in the language already touched, is there a maintained community-authored curriculum ending at professional practice, do students and professionals ask questions about the same files in the same place, and at what rung does the licence force a switch. A tool can have a beautiful tutorial and fail the first two questions (TUKAcad's Learning Edition, SketchUp Free, every AI image tool), and a tool can pass them and still plateau on licence alone (MATLAB Home, Wolfram Cloud Basic, Fusion personal). The practical rule from the expertise literature is to adopt the expert's procedural habits in the first week, because practised patterns persist and the switch to an expert technique is otherwise all-or-nothing rather than incremental.

For apparel pattern structure, start in **Seamly2D**: it is free, it is the Mathematica-like door, and a first skirt or bodice block built from the learner's own measurement file is already a measurement-driven, regradable object that a bespoke tailor could use ([Seamly2D README](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/blob/develop/.github/README.md)) [verified]. Take the SeamlyME measurement names from one published drafting system and stay with it, store ease and length once in the variables table so changes propagate, and build the seam-walking check block from the first garment rather than later, because the system will not do it for you. Expect the artifact to be portable only within one fork's version window, and export DXF-AAMA/ASTM with its `.rul` file early, not because you need a factory but because learning the interchange boundary is part of learning the discipline. If the learner is a programmer, start in **FreeSewing** instead or in parallel: `npx @freesewing/new-design`, the three-part bib tutorial, the best-practices guide's rule to "embrace percentages", and the doll and giant tests that ship in the development environment ([FreeSewing best practices](https://freesewing.dev/guides/best-practices/)) [search summary] give the most Lean-like discipline available in fashion, and a first design that inherits from Brian or Bella via `from:` is structurally identical to the catalogue's professional blocks. Treat **GarmentCode** as the system to read and contribute to rather than to learn on: it is where the formal and AI frontiers meet, its MIT licence and JSON spec make it the likeliest substrate for whatever product eventually assembles the ladder, and its research-grade install and dozen components make it a poor first rung. If the destination is a conventional factory workflow on rule-table CAD rather than made-to-measure, **PAD System Basic at US$15/month** is the only affordable industrial rung whose files are the professional edition's files ([PAD pricing](https://www.padsystem.com/content/pricing)) [search summary]; avoid TUKAcad's Learning Edition for anything meant to last, and treat Tailornova, PatternLab and Bootstrap as places to buy a pattern, not to learn one.

For 3D, use **CLO** if eligible for the student rate (US$25/month) or willing to pay the Individual rate (US$50/month), and **Marvelous Designer** at US$8.25/month if the interest tilts toward CG or the budget is tight, since the simulation layer is the same and the production features can be added later ([CLO student discounts](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115013366988-Does-CLO-offer-student-discounts); [AAP](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260709ae01014/)) [search summary]. The first exercise should be to import the Seamly2D or FreeSewing DXF, stitch it, simulate it, and run Check Sewing Length and the graded-size review before judging the drape; that sequence is the professional's movement pattern, and because the solver absorbs mismatches as wrinkles it is the one habit the tool will never enforce. Replace default fabrics with measured ones as soon as possible and learn to read a tension map. Consider **Browzwear's Freelancer plan** only when the target is enterprise technical development, where it is the preferred dialect, and take the free Browzwear University 101–301 sequence first regardless. Use **Blender** when the goal is film or game garments or presentation finish, not for pattern engineering. For the adjacent fields the same logic gives three clean choices: **Rhino's US$195 educational licence with Grasshopper** for anything hard-goods (jewellery, footwear lasts and soles, hardware), the one case in the survey where the student seat is the perpetual, commercially usable professional program; **Onshape Free** for mechanical work, where the sketch constraints a beginner learns run on the same D-Cubed solver as NX; and a deliberate effort, in either, to learn the sketch-then-feature mental model early, since that paradigm rather than any feature is the documented novice hurdle.

| Path | First tool and cost | First exercise | Professional habit to adopt immediately | Verify before paying |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apparel, non-programmer | Seamly2D (free) | Skirt block from own measurement file | Formula-based seam walk block; standard measurement names; DXF-AAMA + .rul export | Current release and macOS/Windows build status (release pages were blocked) |
| Apparel, programmer | FreeSewing (free, Node 20) | Tutorial bib, then a design `from:` Brian or Bella | Percentages not absolutes; doll and giant tests; version control | Codeberg docs versus the 18-month-old GitHub mirror |
| Apparel, factory-bound | PAD System Basic (US$15/mo) | Graded block within the 20-piece cap | Rule-table grading; marker awareness | Trial terms and Basic-to-Standard upgrade path |
| 3D garment | CLO Student US$25/mo or Individual US$50/mo; MD Student US$8.25/mo | Import own DXF, stitch, simulate | Check Sewing Length; 2D Grading Review; measured fabrics; tension maps | Help-centre prices are undated; MD student plan restrictions unconfirmed |
| Enterprise technical design | Browzwear Freelancer US$75/mo after free BWU 101–301 | Rebuild a CLO garment in VStitcher | FAB/U3M fabric data; release-stitch fit checks | What Freelancer includes (Lotta, Stylezone, library) |
| Hard goods and wearables | Rhino edu US$195 + Grasshopper | Graded ring or last variant | Script components when the graph grows | Commercial-use clause with your reseller |
| Research and AI frontier | GarmentCode (free) | Run `t-shirt.yaml` through the GUI, then edit a component | Treat the simulator as the checker; read Design2GarmentCode and ChatGarment | Configurator uptime; export formats beyond JSON |

Two honest warnings close the recommendations. The fashion ladder has no Mathlib: the free formal systems do not share a format, the industrial systems accept only graded geometry, and documented professional adoption of Seamly2D, FreeSewing and GarmentCode is thin or self-reported, so a learner who builds expertise in them is betting on an ecosystem rather than joining an established one, with CLO as the bridge the job market actually recognises. And none of the AI tools should be a foundation: use image generators for ideation if useful, treat anything that promises "production-ready patterns" from a sketch as unverified until a customer case with pattern-level detail exists, and watch Style3D, Lectra's Apogy and the GarmentCode front-ends, because the components of a real ladder (a cheap measurement-parametric engine, a DSL as the expert layer, program synthesis as the entry rung, a simulator as the checker) all exist in 2026 and nobody has assembled them into one product with a consumer floor.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing this survey changes is where to look for the ladder. It is tempting to look at tutorials, free tiers and student prices, but those are the social and economic conditions under which people climb, not the ladder itself; the ladder is a shared representation plus a level-blind arbiter, and in fashion the arbiter is split in a way it is not in mathematics or mechanical CAD. The cloth simulator, which looks like the kernel, is a Mathematica-type evaluator that turns a novice's error into wrinkles, while the real strictness sits in grading rules, exchange formats and fabric-digitisation standards that a beginner can avoid for months. That is why the coaching analogy bites harder in fashion than in Lean: Lean's kernel is the coach and refuses the wrong movement, whereas CLO will happily simulate it, so the professional's procedural checks have to be installed as habits from the first garment rather than discovered at the professional transition.

The second shift is that the frontier is moving toward the formal end rather than away from it. The industry's own research labs (Style3D, Stanford, MPI, ETH) chose a typed DSL as the target for generative models because a program can be checked by a simulator and a picture cannot, the same reason every medal-level IMO system targets Lean. That makes GarmentCode, not any image tool, the object to watch, and it makes the open formula and code systems a sensible place to build intuition even though their professional ecosystems are thin: the representation a learner internalises there, a garment as a function of a body, is the one the next generation of tools is converging on. The gap that remains is institutional rather than technical. Fashion has no Mathlib, no shared formal format into which a first-year's block can be upstreamed, and until one exists the ladder will be climbable within a single system and lossy between systems, which is a reasonable thing to know before choosing where to put the first rung.
