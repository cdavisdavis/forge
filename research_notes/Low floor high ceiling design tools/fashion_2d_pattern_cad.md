# Fashion 2D pattern-making CAD and "patterns as code" systems, evaluated for "low floor, high ceiling" (state as of 18 October 2026)

*Method and limits of this note.* Vendor, school and documentation hosts (lectra.com, tukatech.com, optitex.com, padsystem.com, audaces.com, seamly.io, freesewing.dev, arts.ac.uk, fitnyc.edu, tandfonline.com, theinterline.com, codeberg.org and others) were blocked by this session's egress policy, so vendor and school pages were read only through search-engine summaries of those pages, and every vendor fact below should be treated as "as summarised from the cited page", not as a direct quotation. The only hosts readable directly were GitHub raw files, GitLab, the npm registry and PyPI, which is why FreeSewing, Valentina, Seamly2D and GarmentCode are documented from primary files and the proprietary tools are not. The session's web-search budget was exhausted before searches on Grafis, StyleCAD's official pricing, Sewist, Bunka's CAD vendor, Parsons' labs, the AccuMark/Optitex grading data model, Modaris V2026.1's feature list, FreeSewing's professional adoption, and the content of The Interline's March 2026 patternmaking article and the TailorCoPilot paper could be run. Those are recorded as gaps rather than guessed.

## Key question 1: Industry 2D pattern CAD (Lectra Modaris, Gerber AccuMark, Optitex PDS, Assyst, TUKAcad, Audaces, PAD System, StyleCAD, Wild Ginger Cameo): cost, beginner/student on-ramp, internal model

### Takeaway
Every tier-one industrial 2D system (Modaris, AccuMark, Optitex, Assyst, Audaces Pro) is quote-priced, sold on 1–3-year subscriptions or per-module perpetual licences, and reaches beginners almost only through institutional "partner school" programmes; the only industrial-grade systems with a self-serve on-ramp under US$50/month are TUKAcad Learning Edition (US$19/mo, but file-incompatible with the professional edition) and PAD System Basic (US$15/mo, same engine as the US$45 Standard tier, 20-piece cap). Internally these are point-and-curve drafting systems with grading expressed as per-point rule tables (point-to-point, table or formula grading), with "parametric" capability sold as a premium add-on (Modaris Expert, Assyst Smart.Pattern macros) rather than as the base representation.

### Inferences
- The industrial tier has no "same tool, elementary use" path for an individual: the beginner's realistic route is an institution that holds a departmental licence (Optitex, Lectra partner schools) or a paid short course that lends a licence for its duration. Self-serve access that is continuous with the professional edition exists only at PAD System (same engine, piece-count cap) and, with a hard file-format wall, at Tukatech.
- TUKAcad's Learning Edition is explicitly *not* structurally continuous with the professional product: no DXF, and LE files cannot be opened in the Professional Edition. A learner's bodice block there is a dead end by design.
- Across the proprietary systems the base representation is drawn geometry with grading stored as per-point rule tables; "parametric" behaviour (Modaris Expert, Assyst macros, Modaris formula grading) is an add-on layer applied to that geometry, which is why academic authors classify Modaris as "hybrid/track-based" rather than parametric.

### Gaps
- No official list price for Modaris, AccuMark, Optitex, Assyst, Audaces Pro or StyleCAD could be confirmed; all dollar figures above are third-party and conflicting.
- No official student/academic price or eligibility terms were found for Lectra, Optitex or Assyst; only the existence of partner-school programmes is documented.
- The internal grading/data model of AccuMark PDS (rule tables, storage areas, MTM alteration rules) and Optitex (grade points, base size) could not be documented from vendor sources because those searches were not run before the budget was exhausted and the help sites were blocked.
- Grafis (German construction-recording CAD that re-runs recorded drafting steps on new measurements, with an education edition) was identified late via its help pages but could not be researched; it is a strong candidate for the "most Lean-like industrial tool" slot and should be covered in a follow-up.
- Modaris V2026.1 feature list and the status of "Modaris Expert MTM" were not confirmed.

## Key question 2: Open and parametric systems (Seamly2D, Valentina, FreeSewing, GarmentCode, Tailornova/PatternFast, Sewist, PatternLab London, Bootstrap Fashion, Wild Ginger PatternMaster, PatternMaker, Fittingly Sew): representation, professional use, learning paths

### Takeaway
Three systems represent a pattern as a formal object rather than as drawn geometry: Seamly2D/Valentina (an XML pattern file whose every point is a formula over named measurements and earlier objects, evaluated by a formula parser), FreeSewing (a pattern is a JavaScript design whose `draft()` computes points and Bezier paths from a named-measurement set, published as npm packages and re-drafted per body), and GarmentCode (a Python DSL of Edges, Panels, Components and Interfaces, from ETH Zurich). All three are free, measurement-driven, and reuse the same object for a beginner's first bodice as for an expert's garment; documented professional use is thin and anecdotal (bespoke/MTM makers for Seamly2D, none found for FreeSewing, research only for GarmentCode). The consumer generators (Tailornova/PatternFast, PatternLab London, Bootstrap Fashion, PatternMaster) are template systems whose parametric logic the user never sees or edits.

### Cited Findings

**Seamly2D (fork of Valentina, 2017)**
- The project README describes Seamly2D as open-source patternmaking software "to democratize fashion", GPLv3+, for Windows 10/11 (x64 and ARM64), macOS 13 Ventura through "macOS Tahoe 26", and Linux (Flatpak via Flathub, AppImage); "Each Seamly2D pattern can read multi-size measurement files for standardized sizes *and* read individual measurement files for custom-fit"; the formula engine is QMuParser; the user wiki is marked "needs updating" while Doxygen developer docs are "current" — [Seamly2D README (develop branch)](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/blob/develop/.github/README.md)
- Academic classification: "Seamly2D and Valentina are 2D parametric CAD systems"; points are placed by distance and angle from a datum and the user supplies those distances as variables/formulas; "the traditional approach allows quicker construction but becomes time-consuming for significant pattern changes, whereas the parametric approach, although slower to construct, offers editable blocks for efficient modification"; parametric blocks code "each element of the pattern as a discrete point linked to input data" — [Gill et al. 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17543266.2023.2260829)
- Measurements live in separate files managed by SeamlyME (formerly Tape): individual files for custom fit, multisize files for standard sizes; templates pre-select the measurement names used by a commercial drafting book/system, and the manual advises using the same pattern system in SeamlyME and Seamly2D — [Seamly wiki: Tape measurements](https://wiki.seamly.io/wiki/UserManual:Tape_measurements); [SeamlyME manual (PDF)](https://static.wikitide.net/seamlywiki/f/fa/SeamlyME_Manual.pdf); [Seamly2D QuickStart (PDF)](https://static.wikitide.net/seamlywiki/a/a7/Seamly2D-QuickStart-en.pdf)
- Values such as garment length or ease can be stored once in the Measurements → Variables Table so that a change propagates through the whole pattern (commercial blog, treat its income claims with caution) — [Digitalise Fashion Design](https://digitalisefashiondesign.com/what-nobody-tells-you-about-learning-seamly2d-2/); a pattern-drafting teacher's post argues Seamly2D changes how drafting can be taught because every point is a formula — [Kehinde Oni, Substack](https://kehindeoni.substack.com/p/i-just-discovered-seamly2d-and-it)
- Professional use (forum, self-reported): one member uses it professionally for bespoke jeans and trousers; another with a 30-year made-to-measure career says they moved from hand drafting to Seamly2D because of how it creates multi-size grades — [Seamly forum: "Do you manage to work professionally in seamly2d?"](https://forum.seamly.io/t/do-you-manage-to-work-professionally-in-seamly2d/6277)
- File format: the pattern format is XML (.val historically, now .sm2d); a moderator states "there is no official spec, and the schemas can and do change at any time" — [Seamly forum: .sm2d schema](https://forum.seamly.io/t/looking-for-official-documentation-or-xml-schema-for-sm2d-files/15403); a third-party Java library parses Valentina/Seamly2D formulas (the `length` attribute of drawing objects) into an operator-precedence tree — [KimMF/FormulaParser](https://github.com/KimMF/FormulaParser)
- Fork history: Valentina was started in 2013 by Roman Telezhinsky and Susan Spencer; after internal conflict Telezhinsky kept Valentina and Spencer's side became Seamly2D (December 2017); both still share much code — [LibreArts, Dec 2017](https://librearts.org/2017/12/valentina-seamly2d/); post-fork Valentina files may not open in Seamly2D because the schema diverged, while older Seamly files open in Valentina; terminology diverged ("notch" vs "passmark") — [Seamly forum: Valentina to Seamly](https://forum.seamly.io/t/get-patterns-from-valentina-to-seamly/6417)
- The community is discussing a "new Seamly pattern drafting system" and cites the Gill et al. paper as relevant — [Seamly forum discussion](https://forum.seamly.io/t/discussion-about-a-new-seamly-pattern-drafting-system/14012/10)

**FreeSewing (patterns as JavaScript)**
- The GitHub repository now carries only a notice: "FreeSewing has moved to Codeberg" (codeberg.org/freesewing/freesewing), with an explanatory blog post — [freesewing/freesewing on GitHub](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing); [FreeSewing blog "us-and-eu"](https://freesewing.eu/blog/us-and-eu)
- Release state: the npm registry lists `@freesewing/core` latest = 4.10.2, published 2026-09-13, with releases 4.5.0 (2026-02-22), 4.6.0, 4.7.x, 4.8.0, 4.9.0 (2026-05-25), 4.10.0 (2026-06-19), 4.10.1 (2026-08-16); repository field "codeberg:freesewing/freesewing"; 4.0.0 shipped 2024-04-01 per the changelog — [npm: @freesewing/core](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@freesewing/core); [FreeSewing CHANGELOG (GitHub mirror)](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/CHANGELOG.md)
- The core README calls it "a library for creating made-to-measure sewing patterns", MIT-licensed, maintained by Joost De Cock and funded by patrons; "a *batteries-included* toolbox for parametric design of sewing patterns"; a dev environment launches with `npx @freesewing/studio` and needs NodeJS v20 — [@freesewing/core README](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/packages/core/README.md)
- Representation: a design's draft function receives `{ Point, points, Path, paths, Snippet, snippets, options, measurements, macro, sa, complete, paperless, store, part }`; a Pattern "is a *constructor* that takes the user's settings as input", has a `draft()` method, and renders to SVG via `render()` or to any front end via `getRenderProps()`; `measurements` and `options` are the settings defined by each design; since v3 a pattern can be drafted for multiple settings sets at once ("multisets") — [FreeSewing design guide](https://freesewing.dev/guides/designs/); the Pattern object is "the parametric blueprint that when instantiated with a user's settings can draft a bespoke sewing pattern" — [FreeSewing Pattern API](https://freesewing.dev/reference/api/pattern/)
- Measurements are declared per part by official name (e.g. `measurements: ['head']`), and the docs insist on reusing the standard measurement names so bodies are reusable across designs; "The FreeSewing core library does not care" about names, the ecosystem does — [Tutorial: adding measurements](https://freesewing.dev/tutorials/pattern-design/part2/adding-measurements/)
- Inheritance: garments are built on blocks in code. Aaron's front part is declared `from: base` imported from `@freesewing/brian` (the menswear body block), inherits Brian's options, and defines its own as percentages with min/max (e.g. `chestEase: { pct: 8, min: 0, max: 20, ...pctBasedOn('chest') }`) — [designs/aaron/src/front.mjs](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/designs/aaron/src/front.mjs); the catalogue lists blocks as first-class designs, e.g. Bella "a womenswear bodice block" (difficulty 3, tags blocks/tops) and Brian "a basic body block for menswear", among 63 entries including test designs — [config/software/designs.json](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/config/software/designs.json); [@freesewing/brian README](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/designs/brian/README.md)
- Learning path: a three-part tutorial (prerequisites via `npx @freesewing/new-design`; parametric design of a bib from `head` circumference; then translation, laser cutting, paperless, and integration) — [FreeSewing pattern design tutorial](https://freesewing.dev/tutorials/pattern-design/); the tutorial code constructs the neck opening by iterating `do { ... delta = paths.neck.length() - target ... } while (Math.abs(delta) > 1)` until the curve length matches the target derived from the measurement — [Tutorial part 3: paperless](https://freesewing.dev/tutorials/pattern-design/part3/paperless/)

### Inferences
- Structural continuity is strongest in FreeSewing: a beginner's tutorial bib and the professional-grade Brian/Bella blocks are the same kind of object (an npm design with a `draft()` function), garments literally inherit from blocks via `from:`, and the same test harness drafts both across every size and the doll/giant extremes. The cost is that the "floor" is a JavaScript toolchain (Node v20, `npx`), not a drafting canvas.
- Seamly2D/Valentina offer the most Mathematica-like path: an interactive canvas where every click produces a formula, so a novice's first skirt is already a measurement-driven, regradable object; the price is a pattern-file format with no published spec and two diverging schemas, so the formal artifact is portable only within one fork's version window.
- GarmentCode is the most Lean-like in representation (typed Edges/Panels/Components/Interfaces, programs in Python, machine-generated datasets) but has no practitioner on-ramp today: its GUI configurator has been offline since April 2025 and its audience is graphics/ML researchers.
- Consumer generators (Tailornova, PatternLab, Bootstrap) have a very low floor and zero ceiling in the relevant sense: the parametric logic exists but is the vendor's, so nothing the user learns or builds carries into a professional system except the exported geometry.

### Gaps
- No independent, named case of a production brand using FreeSewing, GarmentCode, or Valentina was found; Seamly2D's professional use is self-reported in its own forum.
- "Sewist CAD" could not be documented: results only confirm Sewist as a custom-pattern site a user preferred over Tailornova; its pricing and representation were not searched before the budget ran out.
- FreeSewing's current docs on Codeberg were unreachable; the documentation quoted comes from the GitHub mirror and freesewing.dev paths, which the npm data show are at least 18 months behind the latest core release in version terms (mirror says 4.0.0; npm says 4.10.2). Feature statements should be checked against freesewing.dev.
- Seamly2D's current release number and date were not captured (GitHub API and release pages were blocked); the README's support for macOS 26 indicates maintenance in 2025–26.

## Key question 3: Is there a Lean-like "kernel" in fashion, a mechanical check that seam lengths match, notches align and pieces assemble? Where does it live?

### Takeaway
No mainstream 2D system has a kernel in the Lean sense (a small trusted checker that refuses an inconsistent pattern). What exists is (a) measurement/inspection tools in 2D CAD that report seam lengths for a human to compare, (b) "assembly" or "walk" functions in Modaris and similar that juxtapose pieces for visual checking, (c) in code-based systems, consistency enforced by the author's own constraint loops and by continuous-integration tests that draft every design for every size, and (d) 3D simulation (CLO, Browzwear, Optitex 3D, Modaris 3D), which is the de facto assembly check because stitching the pieces exposes mismatches; that handoff is covered by the separate 3D researcher.

### Cited Findings
- Walking seams is defined by practitioners as pivoting one piece along its partner so seamlines stay together and checking whether the ends match; "pieces sewn one-to-one should be equal", intentional ease (sleeve cap) is measured the same way; curves like armholes are hard to judge by eye, so walking is the reliable check — [Minerva Patterns: truing and walking seams](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/truing-and-walking-seams-why-pattern-accuracy-matters); [Threads: How to walk the seamline](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/2021/02/05/how-to-walk-the-seamline); [Fashion-Incubator: how to walk a pattern](https://fashion-incubator.com/how-to-walk-a-pattern-pt-2/)
- Modaris provides an "F8 – Seam length" measurement that lists distances between points in a spreadsheet and an assembly function that places pieces on a host sheet so seams can be matched and checked (textbook description, third-party hosted) — [Stott, Modaris textbook excerpt](https://vdoc.pub/documents/pattern-cutting-for-clothing-using-cad-how-to-use-lectra-modaris-pattern-cutting-software-1bh5c02hsdb8); Lectra's own claim for Modaris Expert is that it lets patternmakers "error-proof the pattern creation process" through parametric capabilities (marketing) — [Lectra Modaris](https://www.lectra.com/en/fashion/products/modaris)
- Optitex 2D exposes seam-allowance widths and segment lengths for all pieces in one view, for the user to compare and adjust — [Optitex help: seam values](https://help.optitex.com/Optitex_2D/Viewing_the_Seam_Values_for_all_Pieces.htm)
- Assyst offers Smart.Measure to "quickly measure lines and curves" (a reporting tool, not a checker) — [Assyst.CAD](https://www.assyst.de/en/products/cad/)
- Seamly2D has no built-in seam-walk; a user built a dedicated empty block, placed last in creation order so all variables exist, containing lines whose formulas are differences between paired curve lengths (armscye vs sleeve cap), plus lines reporting each seam's total length; other members suggested a future formal tool with a formula dialog — [Seamly forum: a method to "walk the seams"](https://forum.seamly.io/t/a-method-to-walk-the-seams-and-check-seam-lengths/7689); forum replies state you cannot set a curve's exact length by formula because many curves share endpoints and length, so matching is by iteration — [Seamly forum: make a curve with exact measurements](https://forum.seamly.io/t/make-a-curve-with-exact-measurments/6067); a trouser inside-leg match was achieved by constructing a midpoint and perpendicular and adjusting until the difference was about 0.09 cm — [Seamly forum: matching curved seam lengths](https://forum.seamly.io/t/matching-curved-seam-lengths/3001); a user reported a roughly 1 mm discrepancy between a formula value and the resulting curve length — [Seamly forum: point not in the correct place](https://forum.seamly.io/t/point-not-in-the-correct-place/16221)
- FreeSewing's core gives `Path.length()` and the official tutorial uses it in a feedback loop that resizes the neck curve until its length is within 1 mm of the target — [FreeSewing tutorial: paperless](https://freesewing.dev/tutorials/pattern-design/part3/paperless/); every design ships an auto-generated shared test that runs config, i18n, drafting and sampling tests — [designs/brian/tests/shared.test.mjs](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/designs/brian/tests/shared.test.mjs); the drafting test instantiates the pattern for every adult cisFemale and cisMale size in `@freesewing/models` and for the doll and giant families, calls `draft().render()`, and fails if any error is logged — [tests/designs/drafting.mjs](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/tests/designs/drafting.mjs); the sampling test samples every option and the full size range for humans, dolls and giants — [tests/designs/sampling.mjs](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/tests/designs/sampling.mjs)
- GarmentCode's library includes an `Interface` type among its base types alongside Edge, Panel and Component, i.e. stitching connections are first-class objects in the program — [PyPI: pygarment](https://pypi.org/project/pygarment/)
- A review of parametric clothing-pattern CAD methodology (International Journal of Clothing Science and Technology, 2024) and an earlier "constructive approach toward a parametric pattern-making model" exist as academic treatments of formal pattern models — [ResearchGate: review of parametric clothing pattern CAD](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378179731_A_review_of_parametric_clothing_pattern_CAD_software_methodology); [ResearchGate: constructive approach](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258195889_A_constructive_approach_toward_a_parametric_pattern-making_model)
- 2D-to-3D handoff: CLO imports and exports 2D patterns as DXF (AAMA/ASTM) — [CLO support: 2D pattern DXF import/export](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000493067-2D-Pattern-DXF-Import-Export); FIT's technical-design course pairs Gerber AccuMark PDS patternmaking with "3D virtual fitting in Browzwear" for "garment fit analysis and pattern corrections" — [FIT catalog: TC courses](https://catalog.fitnyc.edu/undergraduate/courses/tc/)

### Inferences
- The closest thing to a kernel today is FreeSewing's CI: it does not prove seams match, but it mechanically guarantees that every published design drafts without error for every standard body and for bodies 10x smaller and 3x larger. That is a type-check, not a proof of assembly.
- In every system, seam-length equality is an *author-imposed* invariant (a formula, a loop, a visual walk), not a *system-enforced* one. The only place the full assembly is mechanically exercised is 3D simulation, which is why industry treats 3D as the validation step and 2D as authoring.
- GarmentCode's explicit Interface objects make it the design most amenable to a real checker (lengths of connected edges are available to the program), but that is an inference from its type system, not a documented feature.

### Gaps
- Whether Modaris Expert or AccuMark include any automatic seam-length mismatch warning (rather than a measurement report) could not be confirmed from vendor documentation.
- GarmentCode's actual behaviour when stitched edges differ in length (whether it errors, adds gathering, or ignores) was not verified; the paper was not readable.
- No source was found describing notch-alignment checking in any 2D system.

## Key question 5: Grading and made-to-measure from the start (which systems let a simple pattern scale correctly across sizes), and what is lost moving from a consumer tool to a professional one

### Takeaway
Only the measurement-driven systems (Seamly2D/Valentina, FreeSewing, GarmentCode) make a beginner's first block regradable by construction: swapping the measurement file or set re-drafts it, and FreeSewing additionally tests that scaling to doll and giant bodies holds. Among affordable proprietary tools, PAD System's US$15 tier includes the same grading as the professional tier, while TUKAcad's Learning Edition caps grading at 4 sizes and blocks file transfer. What is lost on migration is the grading itself: industry exchange formats carry geometry plus per-point rules only when both sides speak DXF-AAMA/ASTM with a .rul file, consumer tools export PDF or plain DXF, and the proprietary native formats (Modaris, AccuMark, Optitex, .sm2d) do not interchange.

### Cited Findings
- Seamly2D patterns read multi-size measurement files for standardised sizes and individual files for custom fit, so one pattern regrades by changing the measurement file — [Seamly2D README](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/blob/develop/.github/README.md); Valentina states the same for "standard sizing tables or an individual's set of measurements" — [Valentina README](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina/-/blob/develop/README.md); a 30-year MTM professional cites multi-size grading as the reason for adopting Seamly2D — [Seamly forum](https://forum.seamly.io/t/do-you-manage-to-work-professionally-in-seamly2d/6277)
- FreeSewing has no size concept: every draft is from a measurement set, and `Pattern.sample()` drafts multiple variants stacked together, by option, by one measurement varied 90%–110% in 10 steps, or across models (bodies) — [FreeSewing Pattern.sample()](https://freesewing.dev/reference/api/pattern/sample); [Pattern.sampleMeasurement()](https://freesewing.dev/reference/api/pattern/samplemeasurement); its best practices demand percentage-based options and multiples of `sa` precisely so patterns survive scaling to doll (1/10) and giant (3x) bodies — [FreeSewing best practices](https://freesewing.dev/guides/best-practices/); CI drafts every design for every size of `@freesewing/models` adult cisFemale/cisMale plus doll and giant — [tests/designs/drafting.mjs](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/tests/designs/drafting.mjs)
- GarmentCode drafts garment programs against body-measurement presets and was used to generate GarmentCodeData, a dataset of 3D made-to-measure garments with sewing patterns (ECCV 2024) — [PyPI: pygarment](https://pypi.org/project/pygarment/); [GarmentCodeData](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17609v1)
- PAD System's US$15 Basic and US$45 Standard tiers both include grading; Basic is capped at 20 pattern pieces — [PAD System pricing](https://www.padsystem.com/content/pricing)
- TUKAcad Learning Edition allows 4 graded sizes per file, no DXF import/export, and its files will not open in the Professional Edition — [TUKAweb educational plan info](https://www.tukaweb.com/subscriptions/educational_plan_info)
- Wild Ginger sells Grading and "Made to Fit" as separate Cameo modules on top of the US$750–950 base — [Wild Ginger Cameo prices](https://www.wildginger.com/prices/cameo-prices.htm); PatternMaster (consumer) is not licensed for selling patterns — [Wild Ginger store](https://store.wildginger.com/patternmaster-full-versions)

- Interchange and loss: standard DXF describes geometry only; grading travels only in the apparel conventions DXF-AAMA/ASTM with a companion .rul grade-rule file — [Gerber Community](https://gerbertechnology.vanillacommunity.com/discussion/1305/what-is-the-difference-between-a-standard-dxf-file-and-an-astm-aama-dxf-file); [Minerva: rule tables in DXF-AAMA](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/rule-tables-and-grade-rules-inside-dxf-aama-files); [Minerva: AAMA vs ASTM](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/dxf-aama-vs-dxf-astm); if the .rul is missing Optitex loads the pattern without grading — [Optitex import best practices](https://help.optitex.com/1382687/Content/Optitex_2D/Import_best_practices_for_standard_formats.htm); Grafis documents that on export "all pattern stacks are reduced to a base size with grade rules" and the target system recalculates other sizes, so extreme sizes can differ from the original — [Grafis help: export formats](https://help.grafis.de/en-gb/Content/ExportIImport/Exportformate_und_deren_Besonderheiten.htm); [Grafis help: import of grade-rule patterns](https://help.grafis.de/en-gb/Content/ExportIImport/Import_von_Sprungwertschnitten.htm); practitioner guides on DXF breakage — [i-digit](https://www.i-digit.co.uk/post/dxf-import-export-problems), [Alison Hoenes: file formats in fashion](https://alisonhoenes.com/2023/07/file-formats-in-fashion/)

### Inferences
- "Scales correctly from the start" is a property of representation, not of price: the free formula/code systems have it because size is an input, while most paid systems (including the industrial ones) add grading afterwards as rules on finished geometry.
- Migration loss is asymmetric. From a consumer generator you keep only geometry (PDF or plain DXF), losing the parametric logic entirely. From Seamly2D or FreeSewing you keep the logic in a form no industrial system can read; you must re-export graded geometry as DXF-AAMA/ASTM plus .rul (Seamly2D) or re-draft per size and export (FreeSewing), at which point the industrial tool sees rules, not formulas. From TUKAcad LE you keep nothing.
- The formula-based systems are therefore the only ones where early work stays structurally sound *and* editable, but the ecosystem boundary (no shared formal format) is where the Lean analogy breaks: there is no Mathlib equivalent, each system's blocks live only inside it.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how often graded DXF-AAMA exports from Seamly2D or Tailornova import cleanly into AccuMark/Modaris/Optitex; the Grafis warning about recalculated extreme sizes is the only vendor statement found on fidelity loss.
- Made-to-measure modules of Modaris and AccuMark (alteration rules) were not documented because those searches were not executed.

## Key question 6 (synthesis requested in the objective): which systems are most Lean-like (formal, programmatic, exact) and which most Mathematica-like (interactive, accessible, principled)

### Takeaway
Most Lean-like: GarmentCode (typed DSL, programs plus body presets, reproducible datasets) and FreeSewing (designs as versioned npm code with a test harness that drafts every design for every body), with FreeSewing the only one of the two that a motivated beginner can actually enter today. Most Mathematica-like: Seamly2D/Valentina (interactive canvas where every object is a live formula over measurements) and, among paid tools, PAD System (same engine at US$15 and US$45) and Modaris Expert for those inside an institution. Least continuous: TUKAcad Learning Edition (deliberately walled) and the web generators (logic hidden, geometry-only exports).

### Cited Findings
- FreeSewing: a design is a constructor with a `draft()` function over named measurements and percentage options; garments inherit from blocks (`from: base` of Brian); `sample()` drafts across bodies; CI drafts and samples every design for every size and for doll/giant bodies; packages are versioned on npm (4.10.2, Sept 2026) — [design guide](https://freesewing.dev/guides/designs/), [aaron/src/front.mjs](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/designs/aaron/src/front.mjs), [tests/designs/drafting.mjs](https://github.com/freesewing/freesewing/blob/develop/tests/designs/drafting.mjs), [npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@freesewing/core)
- GarmentCode: Edge/Panel/Component/Interface types, YAML design and body presets, published as `pygarment` 2.0.2 (April 2025), GUI configurator offline since April 2025 — [PyPI: pygarment](https://pypi.org/project/pygarment/)
- Seamly2D/Valentina: every point placed by formula over measurements and prior objects; individual and multisize measurement files; QMuParser formula engine; no published file spec — [Gill et al. 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17543266.2023.2260829), [Seamly2D README](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/blob/develop/.github/README.md), [Seamly forum: .sm2d](https://forum.seamly.io/t/looking-for-official-documentation-or-xml-schema-for-sm2d-files/15403)
- PAD System: one product, two tiers (US$15 with 20-piece cap, US$45 unlimited), grading in both — [PAD System pricing](https://www.padsystem.com/content/pricing)
- Modaris: hybrid track-based parametrics, Expert edition sold on "error-proofing", access via partner schools — [Gill et al. 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17543266.2023.2260829), [Lectra Modaris](https://www.lectra.com/en/fashion/products/modaris)
- TUKAcad LE: 14 pieces, 4 sizes, no DXF, files not openable in Pro — [TUKAweb educational plan info](https://www.tukaweb.com/subscriptions/educational_plan_info)
- Web generators: Tailornova templates with PDF-only personal tier — [Tailornova pricing](https://tailornova.com/price); PatternLab blocks from £4.99 as PDF/AI/SVG — [PatternLab London](https://patternlab.london/home/); Bootstrap MTM PDF/PLT/DXF emailed per purchase — [Bootstrap Fashion](https://patterns.bootstrapfashion.com/all-sewing-patterns.html)
- Trade-press framing of the next step is AI-assisted patternmaking, per The Interline's March 2026 feature (title only; content not read) — [The Interline, 11 March 2026](https://www.theinterline.com/2026/03/11/the-next-frontier-for-digital-product-creation-patternmaking-with-ai-assistance/)

### Inferences
- The Lean analogy holds best for FreeSewing on three axes (formal object, versioned ecosystem, mechanical checks in CI) and fails on one (no shared standard between systems, so no "Mathlib" of blocks). The Mathematica analogy holds for Seamly2D on accessibility and principled internals, and fails on portability (unspecified, drifting schema).
- A beginner optimising for structural continuity with professional work today has two defensible routes: Seamly2D (if the goal is bespoke/MTM and the professional target is also Seamly2D or DXF-AAMA export) or PAD System Basic (if the target is a factory workflow on a conventional rule-table CAD); FreeSewing is the right route only for a beginner comfortable with JavaScript tooling.

### Gaps
- Grafis was not assessed and may displace PAD/Modaris as the most Lean-like *industrial* system because it records construction steps rather than geometry; this needs a dedicated follow-up.
- Reddit r/PatternDrafting and Cutter and Tailor forum opinions on starting tools were not retrieved (search not executed), so practitioner sentiment on "start cheap then migrate" rests on the Fashion-Incubator, Pattern School Online and Seamly forum sources above.
