## Key Question 1: CLO 3D (CLO Virtual Fashion): pricing, student/educational licences, trial, learning paths and certification, scripting/API, CLO-SET, industry versus student/independent use

### Takeaway
CLO sells the same desktop application to students (US$25/month or US$225/year, 50% off), individuals (US$50/month or US$450/year) and enterprises (quote-only, with onboarding and a dedicated 3D specialist), with a 14-day trial for individuals/students only; there is no cut-down "student edition", so a student's first garment is built in the professional tool. The on-ramp is CLO Academy (launched October 2024) plus an "Individual User Verification Assessment" certification; the ceiling includes a Python script API and a C++ plug-in SDK, CLO-SET collaboration, DXF-AAMA/ASTM exchange with 2D pattern CAD, and (2026) AI pattern-drafting and texture tools.

### Inferences
- The same binary serves student, individual and enterprise tiers; the tiers differ in price, support and collaboration (CLO-SET) rather than in the modelling/simulation core, so early simple use is structurally continuous with professional use (inference from the plan descriptions; feature-matrix not read directly).
- The Python API's historical narrowness (import/export-centric in 2018) and the Windows-only comment suggest CLO's "ceiling" is primarily interactive, with scripting as an adjunct, which places CLO closer to a Mathematica-like than a Lean-like profile.
- CLO's own 2026 roadmap signal (demand for better 2D CAD) implies the 2D-CAD-to-3D handoff is still a felt seam in professional workflows even for the market leader.

### Gaps

## Key Question 2: Marvelous Designer (same company, CG-focused): how it differs, pricing, use in games and film, learning paths

### Takeaway
Marvelous Designer shares CLO's simulation core but drops apparel-production features (tech packs, DXF-AAMA/ASTM, avatar size grading) in favour of CG features (UV editor, sculpt mode, 3D Pencil); it is priced for individuals at US$39/month or US$280/year, for enterprises at US$199/month or US$2,000/year (since 2 Dec 2025), and since 9 July 2026 for students at US$8.25/month with a four-year window and a three-month free period for new sign-ups until 31 Oct 2026, making it the cheapest fully professional garment simulator on the market.

### Cited Findings
- Personal subscriptions: "$39/month or $280/year" for Windows and macOS; annual plan is a one-time payment not auto-billed — [CG Channel, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/11/clo-virtual-fashion-releases-marvelous-designer-2025-2/); [Digital Production, 15 Apr 2026](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/04/15/marvelous-designer-2026-0-adds-3d-pencil-and-lacing/)
- Enterprise since 2 December 2025: US$199/month or US$2,000/year (Win/Mac), US$2,300/year including the new Linux edition; the standalone (node-locked) enterprise licence was retired — [CG Channel, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/11/clo-virtual-fashion-releases-marvelous-designer-2025-2/)

- Difference from CLO (vendor statement): "the core technology behind the solutions are the same"; Marvelous Designer "is primarily used in the CG, animation, and gaming industries"; "CLO is a more robust solution geared towards the fashion and apparel industries"; CLO-only features include the tech pack, DXF-AAMA/ASTM import/export and avatar size adjustability; MD has a UV editor and sculpt mode — [Marvelous Designer support: difference from CLO](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358306734361-There-is-another-similar-software-called-CLO-what-is-the-difference); [CLO Help Center article 115012666547](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012666547)

### Inferences
- With the same kernel as CLO and a US$8.25/month student price, MD is the lowest-cost route into a professional-grade garment simulator; the structural discontinuity is at the production boundary (no DXF-AAMA/ASTM, no tech pack), not at the physics.
- Because skills, UI and files are largely shared, a student who starts on MD can move to CLO without relearning the simulation layer (inference from vendor and practitioner statements).

### Gaps
- Whether the US$8.25/month student plan carries feature restrictions, watermarks or commercial-use limits was not confirmed (the press release calls the three-month offer "unrestricted"; a follow-up query was prepared but not run).
- No official individual-learner certification for MD was found; only the institutional programme.
- Game-studio usage evidence is weak; the only "EA/Konami" claims come from low-quality mirror sites and were discarded.

## Key Question 3: Browzwear VStitcher and Lotta: industry positioning, Browzwear University and educational access, pricing, fabric digitisation (FAB), open platform/plugins

### Takeaway
Browzwear positions VStitcher as the production/technical-development tool for large apparel teams and Lotta as a block-based design tool; since a February 2026 pricing change individuals can self-serve a Freelancer plan (US$75/month or US$750/year) or Freelancer Plus (US$150/month or US$1,500/year), with Teams at US$3,950/user/year and Enterprise custom. Browzwear University offers free 101/201/301 courses plus a paid membership, FAB digitises fabric into physics parameters shared via the open U3M format, and the tension/pressure maps are the fit-validation instruments.

### Inferences
- Until February 2026 the individual on-ramp to VStitcher ran through time-boxed Indie/trial licences and school partnerships; the Freelancer plan is the first standing, self-serve path for a solo learner, which narrows but does not close the gap with CLO's US$25 student price (no Browzwear student price was found).

## Key Question 7: Is the physics simulator genuinely a "kernel" in the Lean sense: does it mechanically reject inconsistent patterns, and how do professionals use it for validation and fit?

### Takeaway
No. The simulators do not reject inconsistent patterns; they absorb mismatched seam lengths as gathering, puckering or shortened seams and leave detection to optional checkers (CLO's Check Sewing Length flags differences above 3 mm in red but does not block simulation) and to post-hoc instruments (tension/pressure maps, stitch release, fit on sized avatars). The kernel is therefore a physical consistency oracle of the Mathematica kind (always evaluates, sometimes to a visibly wrong object), not a type checker of the Lean kind.

### Cited Findings
- CLO's Check Sewing Length tool finds "significant length differences in sewing lines"; "When the Check Sewing Length tool is activated, the Sewing Lines with the sewing length difference larger than 3mm will be highlighted in red" and bolded; thresholds can be absolute or a ratio — [CLO Help Center: Check Sewing Length](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012226027-Check-Sewing-Length); [CLO Help Center: 2D Tool Check Sewing Length](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000495467--2D-Tool-Check-Sewing-Length)
- A user comment on that article reports a seam flagged red although the 3D lengths were identical and concludes "sewing length is calculated based on 2D line length" (user inference) — same article
- CLO staff advice in a community thread: "make sure the lines to be sewn have the same or similar length, since a shorter line shortens the connected one"; the user's workaround was "Making the 2D line length longer to get the 3D length of the original 2D line" — [CLO community: 3D line length too short](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/ja/community/posts/900002149046-3D-line-length-is-way-too-short-compared-to-the-2D-line-length-how-to-change-the-3D-line-length)
- Grading case: pocket-bag stitch lines that did not grade with the body seam produced "puckering and stitching length not matching" in larger sizes; the simulation ran anyway and the thread is unresolved — [CLO community: Grading sewing length issues](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/ja/community/posts/29039442377497-Grading-Sewing-length-issues-on-certain-pieces)

### Inferences
- The "kernel" analogy holds only in the weaker sense that the garment will not look right unless the pattern is right; the simulator always produces some drape, so a novice's error manifests as puckering, shortened seams or exploded geometry rather than as a refusal. Professionals compensate with procedural checks (Check Sewing Length, graded-size review, tension maps, digitised fabric inputs) that a beginner can skip.
- This makes the 3D garment systems structurally continuous (one kernel, same files) but epistemically permissive; the strictness comes from the 2D pattern CAD side (grading, seam-allowance rules, DXF-AAMA/ASTM) and from fabric digitisation standards, not from the solver.

## Key Question 9 (cross-cutting flag requested in the objective): which systems are most Lean-like versus most Mathematica-like

### Takeaway
None of the 3D garment systems is Lean-like at the solver level; the closest to strict/checked/programmatic are CLO (Python API, C++ SDK, Check Sewing Length, 2D Grading Review, DXF-AAMA/ASTM) and Browzwear (measured FAB/U3M fabric physics, tension/pressure validation, API-based Open Platform, block-based Lotta), while Marvelous Designer, Blender add-ons and Style3D's AI-first tooling are the most Mathematica-like (interactive, forgiving, still principled).

### Cited Findings
- Programmatic surface: CLO documents a Python Script API and C++ plug-in SDK — [developer.clo3d.com](https://developer.clo3d.com/); Browzwear's Open Platform is reached through its API (FNX integration) and U3M — [Browzwear blog: FNX integration](https://browzwear.com/blog/browzwear-fnx-integration); no public API was found for Marvelous Designer, Style3D, Optitex, Modaris 3D, Vidya or TUKA3D in this research
- Checked-ness: CLO's Check Sewing Length (3 mm default) and 2026.1 "2D Grading Review" — [CLO Help Center](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012226027-Check-Sewing-Length); [CLO Newsroom notice 1442](https://clo3d.com/en/resources/notices/1442); Browzwear's Tension & Pressure Map and stitch release — [Browzwear What's New](https://browzwear.com/events/whats-new-in-browzwear-2026.2-edition); Lotta's "workflow based on validated blocks" — [browzwear.com](https://browzwear.com)
- Forgiveness: MD absorbs seam mismatch as wrinkles — [Lynda course](https://origin-www.lynda.com/Marvelous-Designer-tutorials/Sewing-curtain/2825488/2398596-4.html); Simply Cloth lets users draw pattern parts directly on the character in the viewport — [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/01/vjaceslav-tissen-releases-simply-cloth-studio-2-0-for-blender/); Style3D markets AI sketch-to-3D and AI draping — [Style3D AI guide](https://www.style3d.ai/blog/how-can-you-download-and-use-style3d/)
- Same-system continuity for beginners: CLO Student = Individual at half price, 14-day trial — [CLO Help Center](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115013366988-Does-CLO-offer-student-discounts); MD Student US$8.25/month with "unrestricted" free months — [AAP press release](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260709ae01014/); Browzwear Freelancer self-serve — [Browzwear blog](https://browzwear.com/blog/browzwear-pricing-plan); TUKA3D DE US$29/month with trial — [Tukatech](https://tukatech.com/products/tuka3d-de)

### Inferences
- Ranked by Lean-likeness (strict, checked, scriptable): Browzwear ≈ CLO > Optitex/Lectra/AccuMark (strict in 2D, 3D as viewer; no public API found) > TUKA3D > Marvelous Designer > Style3D (AI-first) ≈ Blender add-ons. Ranked by low floor (price and friction): Blender (free) > MD student (US$8.25/mo) > CLO student (US$25/mo) > TUKA3D (US$29/mo) > Browzwear Freelancer (US$75/mo) > Style3D (trial, then unclear) > Optitex/Lectra/Assyst (school licence only).
- The system that best satisfies "beginner uses the professionals' tool simply, and that use is structurally continuous with expert use" is CLO (with MD as the same kernel for CG); Browzwear now qualifies on continuity but its floor is higher and its student path is school-mediated.

### Gaps
- These rankings are inferences from the cited facts; no source ranks the tools on strictness or scriptability, and API scope for CLO and Browzwear could not be verified from primary documentation in this session.
