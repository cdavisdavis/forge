## Key question 1 — Lean 4 and Mathlib: the kernel, why novice and expert proofs are the same object, Mathlib's size, the on-ramps, AI provers, and what educators report

### Takeaway
Lean has the ladder property for a structural reason: every proof, whether a two-line game level or a research formalisation, elaborates to a term that a ~5,000-line C++ kernel (and now an independent Lean-in-Lean checker) must accept, so the checker is blind to the user's level. In 2026 Mathlib is roughly 300k declarations (about 280–290k theorems, 130–140k definitions, ~770 contributors) and the on-ramps (Natural Number Game, Glimpse of Lean, Mathematics in Lean, the live web editor, graded university courses) all run real Lean 4 against that same kernel, so early artifacts are structurally continuous with expert artifacts; the main discontinuity is tooling churn and the gap between a statement and its intended meaning, not the proof object itself.

### Cited Findings

**The trusted kernel (why level does not matter to the checker)**
- Lean's own design talks describe the architecture as "de Bruijn's principle": a small trusted kernel plus external proof and type checkers; "Lean has a small trusted proof checker, and users can export proofs and run external checkers instead of trusting the built-in one." — [Lean at PLDI talk slides, lean-lang.org](https://lean-lang.org/talks/LeanPLDI.pdf); [Lean at Galois slides](https://lean-lang.org/talks/LeanAtGalois.pdf) [search-summary of slides]
- The Lean 4 kernel is "about 5,000 lines of C++" and is trusted because its job is "narrow and deterministic: checking whether a proof term has a given type in the Calculus of Inductive Constructions (it is small, feasible to audit)." — [arXiv 2604.18882 (2026 paper using Lean as certificate checker)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18882) [search-summary; the 5,000-line figure is that paper's estimate, not checked against the repository]
- Recent applied papers treat the kernel as sole arbiter: "every proof is re-checked by a compact, independent kernel in the LCF tradition, so trust rests on that kernel and the stated theorem rather than on the prover that generated the proof." — [arXiv 2608.17880, kernel-checked certificate for Erdős Problem 647](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17880) [search-summary]
- Lean4Lean (Mario Carneiro) is "the first complete typechecker for Lean 4 other than the reference C++ implementation"; it "runs roughly 20% to 50% slower than the original and can check all of mathlib"; a later version also aims to prove properties about the kernel itself. — [arXiv 2403.14064 (Lean4Lean)](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.14064v3) [search-summary]

### Inferences
- The "same kind of object" claim is literally true at the kernel boundary: NNG levels, Glimpse exercises, MIL exercises and Mathlib PRs all elaborate to terms the C++ kernel (or Lean4Lean) must accept. The one caveat is *library* continuity: NNG uses a pedagogical copy of ℕ built "from scratch", so NNG proofs are kernel-valid but not Mathlib-reusable; Glimpse, MIL and the university courses sit directly on Mathlib, so their artifacts are reusable in principle.
- Scale inference: Mathlib theorems grew from ~72.8k (end 2021) to ~288k (2026), about 4×, and definitions ~5.5×; the per-year growth implied by the Feb 2026 study (308k declarations) versus the May 2025 marker (~310k theorems+definitions per the wiki) suggests growth of order 20–30%/year, but the three counts use different classification rules, so the writer should quote a range ("roughly 300k declarations / ~290k theorems in 2026") and name the source for each number.
- Lean's "ladder" is enforced in two directions: downward by the kernel (no level-specific laxity) and upward by extensibility (tactics such as `grind`, Verbose's natural-language tactics and game engines are all written *in Lean*), which is why the beginner layer can be built inside the professional tool rather than beside it.
- The weak rung is churn: monthly releases and Mathlib's pace mean beginner material must be maintained (MIL's PDF is re-released per toolchain version; NNG tracks its own `lean-toolchain`), which is a maintenance cost that design-tool analogues should budget for.

## Key question 2 — Mathematica and the Wolfram Language: the single-symbolic-language claim, the Elementary Introduction, free tiers and student pricing, Raspberry Pi, and the notebook as unifying artifact

### Takeaway
Wolfram's claim to a single language "from arithmetic to research" rests on one representational decision — everything (data, formulas, code, graphics, documents) is a symbolic expression evaluated by one evaluator — so a child's `Graphics[Circle[]]` and a researcher's package are the same kind of object, and the notebook that holds both is itself an expression. The on-ramps are a free online book written in that full language, a free (non-organisational) cloud tier, a free non-commercial Engine, a free Raspberry Pi build of the full system, and student subscriptions; the ladder's weak rung is cost and licence restriction once use becomes organisational or commercial.

### Cited Findings

**The design claim**
- Wolfram's principles page says the language treats "data, formulas, code, graphics, and documents as symbolic expressions", which "makes possible a new level of programming flexibility and power"; companion principles listed are knowledge-based programming, automation, and "maximizing coherence of design." — [wolfram.com/language/principles](https://wolfram.com/language/principles/) [search-summary]
- In his essay on the background and vision of Mathematica, Wolfram presents symbolic expressions as "the reason the system's parts fit together": because every item shares one representation, "a single set of operations applies across all of them." — [writings.stephenwolfram.com, "The Background and Vision of Mathematica"](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/?p=1792) [search-summary]
- In his 2009 Mathematica User Conference keynote Wolfram "called unity the first principle of Mathematica and tied it to symbolic programming." — [Keynote, International Mathematica User Conference 2009](https://publications.stephenwolfram.com/publications/keynote-speech-international-mathematica-user-conference-2009/) [search-summary]
- The Elementary Introduction's chapter 33 demonstrates the principle for beginners: `Graphics[Circle[{0,0}]]` "is another symbolic expression, which just happens to display as a picture of a circle." — [Elementary Introduction, §33 "Expressions and Their Structure"](https://wolfram.com/language/elementary-introduction/33-expressions-and-their-structure.html) [search-summary]
- Wolfram's "for experts" page exists as a separate marketing entry point for the same language (i.e. one product, two doors). — [wolfram.com/language/for-experts](https://wolfram.com/language/for-experts/) [URL from search; content not read]
- *An Elementary Introduction to the Wolfram Language* (Stephen Wolfram; 1st ed. 2015, 3rd ed. 2023) is published free online in full and uses the unrestricted language from its first chapter (arithmetic) onward. — [wolfram.com/language/elementary-introduction](https://www.wolfram.com/language/elementary-introduction/) `[canonical reference; edition dates and preface not re-read this session]`

**The notebook as the unifying artifact**
- Wolfram's framing (via the principles/expressions material above) is that a notebook is itself a symbolic expression, so documents, code and output share one representation; the Raspberry Pi edition explicitly includes "support for notebooks and dynamic constructs", i.e. the same notebook front end at the free end of the ladder. — [wolfram.com/raspberry-pi](https://wolfram.com/raspberry-pi) [search-summary]
- Notebooks have been Mathematica's document format since version 1.0 (1988); Wolfram has written retrospectives on their history and on "computational essays" as the notebook's pedagogical form. — [writings.stephenwolfram.com](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/) `[canonical reference; specific essays not retrieved this session — the planned search could not be run]`

### Inferences
- The structural source of Wolfram's ladder property is representational monism: one data structure (the expression) and one evaluator for every level of use. This is weaker than Lean's guarantee (there is no kernel certifying *correctness*, only a uniform evaluator), but it is the same *shape*: the novice is never handed a reduced object model.
- The on-ramp strategy is "same product, different doors": the Elementary Introduction, the Pi build, Cloud Basic and the student subscription all expose the full language; what varies is price, storage and permitted use, not capability.
- The ladder breaks on licensing rather than on structure: Cloud Basic forbids teaching a course or group research, the Pi build is non-commercial, and Home/Student editions exclude revenue-generating work, so the "tool switch" risk appears at the transition to professional or institutional use.

## Key question 4 — Jupyter/Python, Processing/p5.js, Scratch/Logo, APL and notation: which have the property and which do not, and why

### Takeaway

## Key question 5 — Pedagogy and design literature that names and explains the property

### Takeaway
The property has been named independently in three literatures: HCI tool design (Myers, Hudson & Pausch's "threshold and ceiling", Papert/Resnick's "low floor, high ceiling, wide walls", Kay's "simple things simple, complex things possible"), learning science (Vygotsky's zone of proximal development and scaffolding that is *removed*, Sweller's cognitive load and Kalyuga's expertise-reversal effect, which says novice aids become harmful for experts), and expertise/motor-learning research (Ericsson's deliberate practice and "arrested development", the specificity-of-practice and challenge-point frameworks), while end-user-programming research (Carroll's training wheels, Ko's learning barriers, Nardi, MacLean's gentle slope, Peyton Jones's Excel cliff) documents what happens when the ladder is missing.

### Inferences
- The literatures converge on two separable requirements: a *floor* condition (entry is cheap: Papert/Resnick, Myers' threshold, Sweller's load limits) and a *continuity* condition (what you learn first remains valid: Kalyuga's reversal says the scaffolds must be removable, not baked into a different product; the motor-learning specificity hypothesis says practised patterns persist, so the early pattern should be the expert pattern; Carroll's training wheels show the way to do this is to block, not fork).
- "Wide walls" is a third, orthogonal requirement (many entry projects), evidenced in Lean by eight hosted games and Glimpse's topic tracks, and in Wolfram by the "for experts"/elementary doors into one language.
- Ericsson's "arrested development" and Blackwell's attention-investment model jointly explain the consumer-tool plateau: once a tool makes the easy rung automatic and the next rung expensive, most users rationally stop; a ladder tool has to keep the next rung cheap and visible (progressive disclosure) rather than relying on motivation.
- The HCI "all-or-nothing" adoption finding warns that a ladder needs *small* rungs: if the expert technique is a global switch, users do not climb incrementally.

- Most primary pedagogy texts (Papert 1980; Kay 1977/1993; Victor 2012; Vygotsky 1978; Wood et al. 1976; Carroll 1984; Ericsson 1993/2008; Guadagnoli & Lee 2004; Ko et al. 2004/2011; Peyton Jones et al. 2003; MacLean 1990; Nardi 1993) were cited from canonical bibliographic knowledge and not re-read because of the proxy block and exhausted search budget; quotations from them should be checked before publication.
- No motor-learning source was found that *directly* tests "early bad habits are hard to unlearn"; the nearest evidence is the specificity-of-practice literature and the mixed transfer statistics above.
- No source specifically on "glass ceilings" in *consumer creative apps* (as opposed to spreadsheets) was gathered.

## Key question 6 — A checklist of criteria for the "ladder property", written so it can be applied to fashion CAD, architecture CAD and graphics tools

### Takeaway
Eight criteria, each grounded in a mechanism observed above, separate tools where early simple use is structurally continuous with expert use from tools that merely have an easy start; the decisive ones are a single formal object type across levels and a level-blind checker that validates it.

### Cited Findings
(The criteria are syntheses; each cites the evidence above that motivates it.)

1. **One object type from the first exercise to the frontier.** The novice's artifact and the expert's are the same kind of thing in the same representation. Lean: every proof is a kernel-checked term ([Lean PLDI slides](https://lean-lang.org/talks/LeanPLDI.pdf)); Wolfram: everything is a symbolic expression ([principles](https://wolfram.com/language/principles/)); Processing: a sketch is a Java program ([processing4](https://github.com/processing/processing4)). Counter-cases: Scratch blocks → text ([Weintrop & Wilensky 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3089799)); Excel formulas → VBA ([Peyton Jones et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1145/944705.944721)). *Design-tool test:* does the beginner's pattern/model/shader file open unchanged in the professional's tool and carry the same semantics?

2. **A level-blind checker or kernel.** Something small and trusted decides validity identically for novice and expert, so early work is never "valid for beginners only". Lean's ~5k-line kernel and Lean4Lean ([arXiv 2604.18882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.18882); [lean4lean](https://github.com/digama0/lean4lean)); AI provers rely on it as "a reliable and fast reward signal" ([Aristotle](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01346)). *Design-tool test:* is there a manufacturability/constraint/geometry check that the student's file must pass on the same terms as the professional's (e.g., seam-allowance and grading rules, clash detection, shader compilation)?

3. **Progressive disclosure inside one product, not a separate beginner product.** Scaffolds restrict or annotate the real system and are removable, because novice aids become harmful for experts ([Kalyuga 2007](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/EDC608/Kalyuga2007_Article_ExpertiseReversalEffectAndItsI.pdf)) and blocking functions beats forking the product ([Carroll & Carrithers 1984](https://doi.org/10.1145/358198.358218)). Lean examples: NNG unlocks tactics by level ([NNG4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/NNG4/main/Game.lean)); Verbose's controlled natural language and point-and-click ([verbose-lean4](https://github.com/PatrickMassot/verbose-lean4)). *Design-tool test:* can the beginner mode be switched off without exporting or converting the work?

4. **Artifacts stay valid, reusable and upstreamable as skill grows.** Mathlib accepts "users at all levels" into one repository ([mathlib4 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/README.md)); the Wolfram notebook is the same document at the free Pi rung and the professional rung ([raspberry-pi](https://wolfram.com/raspberry-pi)). Counter-case: notebooks that no longer execute ([Pimentel et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/MSR.2019.00077)). *Design-tool test:* can a first-year's block or component be contributed to the studio library without redrawing?

5. **The ceiling is raised from inside the same language (extensibility).** Tactics, games and natural-language front ends are written in Lean ([lean4game](https://github.com/leanprover-community/lean4game)); Logo's design goal was "no ceiling" ([Logo history](https://el.media.mit.edu/logo-foundation/what_is_logo/history.html)); Myers et al.'s "high ceiling" ([TOCHI 2000](https://doi.org/10.1145/344949.344959)). *Design-tool test:* are the professional's power features (parametrics, scripting, plug-ins) expressed in the same language the beginner already touched, or in a different one?

6. **A canonical graded curriculum authored by the tool's own community, with wide walls.** Lean: NNG → Glimpse (2–3 h) → Mathematics in Lean → Theorem Proving in Lean 4 → university courses ([learn page](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/leanprover-community.github.io/newsite/templates/learn.md); [Glimpse](https://github.com/PatrickMassot/GlimpseOfLean)); Wolfram's Elementary Introduction; MATLAB Onramp. Multiple entry projects (eight hosted Lean games; Glimpse topic tracks) implement Resnick's "wide walls" ([Media Lab article](https://www.media.mit.edu/articles/lifelong-kindergarten-how-to-learn-like-a-kid-by-the-co-creator-of-scratch/)). Difficulty should rise with skill ([Guadagnoli & Lee 2004](https://doi.org/10.3200/JMBR.36.2.212-224)). *Design-tool test:* is there a free, maintained, vendor-or-community-authored sequence that ends at professional practice, with more than one starting project?

7. **One community spanning levels.** Mathlib's Zulip welcomes "all levels of expertise" and the same forum serves MWE debugging via the web editor ([lean4web](https://github.com/leanprover-community/lean4web)); MATLAB Central and Wolfram Community likewise sit beside the product. Counter-case: Scratch's community is separate from any professional one. *Design-tool test:* do students and working professionals ask questions in the same place about the same files?

8. **Cost and licensing that do not force a tool switch at the professional transition.** Lean/Mathlib/Octave are free at every rung; Wolfram and MATLAB are free at the bottom (Cloud Basic, Engine, Pi; Onramp, campus licences) but impose use restrictions and recurring fees as use becomes organisational or revenue-generating ([Cloud Basic terms](https://support.wolfram.com/53990); [MATLAB Home 2026](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/answers/2183326-home-license-in-2026); [perpetual sunset](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/discussions/general/886260-change-in-home-and-student-licenses)). *Design-tool test:* at what rung does the licence require switching products or paying a step-change, and does a compatible free implementation exist?

### Inferences
- Criteria 1–2 are necessary and jointly almost sufficient for the *structural* property the user is asking about; 3–5 describe how the product exposes it; 6–8 are the social and economic conditions under which people actually climb. A tool can satisfy 6–8 and still fail (Scratch), or satisfy 1–5 and plateau on 8 (MATLAB).
- For design fields the "kernel" analogue is whatever institution ultimately decides the artifact is real (the pattern that cuts and sews correctly; the model that passes code/clash checks; the shader that compiles and renders): a ladder tool makes the beginner meet that arbiter from day one, in an elementary way, rather than a simulated or simplified version of it.
- Small rungs matter: the HCI finding that expert techniques are adopted all-or-nothing ([DOI 10.1145/3119928](https://doi.org/10.1145/3119928)) and the attention-investment model ([Blackwell 2002](https://doi.org/10.1109/HCC.2002.1046335)) imply each disclosure step should cost little, or users stop at the first automatic plateau ([Ericsson 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2008.00227.x)).

### Gaps
- The checklist has not been validated against an independent rubric from the literature; it is a synthesis. The closest published rubrics are Myers/Hudson/Pausch's threshold–ceiling–path-of-least-resistance–predictability set and Resnick & Silverman's ten principles, neither of which includes a "checker" criterion — that criterion is the main addition suggested by the Lean case.
